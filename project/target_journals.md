# Target journals and publication strategy

**Decision date:** 2026-09-28  
**Status:** Target selection (submission pending protocol completion and results)

## Primary target journals

The following journals are selected based on fit with the cognitive-science framing, editorial ethos, peer review standards, and audience alignment.

### **1. Cognitive Science (Primary)**

- **Official journal of:** Cognitive Science Society
- **Publisher:** Wiley
- **Scope:** Computational, theoretical, and empirical work on cognition and intelligence
- **Why this journal:**
  - Direct fit for a study of strategic evaluation and generalization in a constrained system
  - Audience includes cognitive scientists, AI researchers, and neuroscientists interested in mechanisms
  - Strong editorial commitment to theory, modeling, and clear reasoning
  - Values honest scope and careful interpretation of computational results
  - Not venue for flashy demos; values intellectual integrity
  - Accepts papers that contribute to understanding cognition, even if not at breakthrough scale

- **Submission process:**
  1. Prepare manuscript following Cognitive Science author guidelines (currently available at [Cognitive Science journal website](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/15516709))
  2. Submit via ScholarOne Manuscripts platform (online submission portal)
  3. Editorial desk review (1–2 weeks): editor determines if in scope and meets basic standards
  4. If passes desk review, sent to 2–3 peer reviewers
  5. Peer review timeline: typically 6–12 weeks; can extend to 4+ months if revisions requested
  6. Editor decision: accept, minor revisions, major revisions, reject
  7. Revision and resubmission (if required): typically 4–8 weeks
  8. Total typical timeline: 4–8 months from submission to first decision; 6–12 months with revisions

- **Key submission items:**
  - Manuscript (IMRaD or similar structure)
  - Figures and tables
  - Supplementary materials (code, data documentation, full protocol, ablation details)
  - Cover letter (1–2 paragraphs explaining the contribution)
  - Suggested reviewers (typically 3–5)
  - Conflict of interest disclosures
  - Data and code availability statement

- **Impact factor:** ~2.5 (moderate, but high prestige in cognitive science community)
- **Open access:** Hybrid (authors can pay for open access)

### **2. eLife (Secondary)**

- **Publisher:** eLife Sciences
- **Scope:** Multidisciplinary peer-reviewed research across life sciences and biomedical science
- **Why this journal:**
  - Open-access, transparent peer review (reviews are published with accepted papers)
  - Strong emphasis on reproducibility, methods clarity, and honest reporting
  - Welcomes computational cognitive and neuroscience work
  - Rewards careful design and thorough ablations over flashy results
  - Editorial ethos values modest, rigorous claims and transparent limitations
  - Accepts papers that contribute incrementally to understanding, not only breakthroughs

- **Submission process:**
  1. Prepare manuscript following eLife author guidelines (available at [eLife website](https://elifesciences.org/))
  2. Submit via eLife submission portal
  3. Editorial assessment (2–4 weeks): editor and in-house reviewer evaluate fit and quality
  4. If passes assessment, sent to external peer reviewers (typically 2)
  5. Peer review timeline: typically 8–12 weeks for review; reviewers encouraged to be thorough
  6. Editor decision: accept, request revisions, desk reject
  7. eLife does not have "minor" vs. "major" revisions; one revision round is typical
  8. Revision and resubmission: 4–6 weeks
  9. Total typical timeline: 4–8 months from submission to first decision; 6–10 months with revisions

- **Key submission items:**
  - Manuscript (structured abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, supplementary figures/tables)
  - Figures and videos (eLife encourages rich multimedia)
  - Supplementary materials (code, data, full protocol)
  - Cover letter (1–2 paragraphs)
  - Author contributions and funding statements
  - Conflict of interest disclosures
  - eLife asks for a "research organism" and "data availability" statement

- **Impact factor:** ~7 (moderate-to-high)
- **Open access:** Yes, fully open access (no author fees at this time)
- **Unique feature:** Published peer reviews (transparency)

### **3. Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience (Tertiary)**

- **Publisher:** MIT Press
- **Scope:** Neural bases of cognition, bridging neuroscience and cognitive science
- **Why this journal:**
  - Strong fit if the contribution emphasizes connectome constraints and neural computation
  - Audience: neuroscientists and cognitive scientists interested in mechanism
  - High impact factor (~4.5), well-respected in both fields
  - Accepts computational and modeling work
  - Expects clear links between neural architecture and cognitive behavior

- **Submission process:**
  1. Prepare manuscript following JCN author guidelines
  2. Submit via Editorial Manager platform
  3. Editorial desk review (1–2 weeks)
  4. If in scope, sent to 3 peer reviewers
  5. Peer review timeline: 6–10 weeks typical; can extend to 4+ months
  6. Editor decision with detailed feedback
  7. Revision and resubmission: 4–8 weeks
  8. Total typical timeline: 4–8 months from submission to first decision; 6–12 months with revisions

- **Key submission items:**
  - Manuscript (Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion)
  - Figures and supplementary figures
  - Supplementary materials (code, data, protocol)
  - Cover letter
  - Suggested reviewers
  - Author contributions and funding
  - Conflict of interest disclosures

- **Impact factor:** ~4.5
- **Open access:** Hybrid

## Submission timeline and strategy

**Before submission:**
- Complete the full protocol and freeze the rubric (not yet done as of 2026-09-28).
- Complete literature review and record in `literature/` files.
- Run the minimum experiment and hold-out evaluation.
- Write the manuscript following the structure in `writing/manuscript_outline.md`.
- Have mentor review the full protocol, results, and manuscript interpretation.
- Ensure code, data, configurations, and checkpoints are documented and reproducible.

**Submission sequence (recommended):**
1. Submit first to **Cognitive Science** (primary target).
2. If rejected or unsuitable feedback, revise and submit to **eLife** (secondary).
3. If eLife rejects, submit to **Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience** (tertiary).
4. Do not submit to multiple journals simultaneously (violates ethical standards).

**Typical total timeline from protocol completion to first acceptance:**
- Manuscript writing: 4–8 weeks.
- First submission to first decision: 4–8 months (desk review + peer review).
- If revisions required: 4–12 additional weeks.
- **Realistic overall: 8–16 months from now**, assuming no major setbacks.

## How to apply (step-by-step)

### Step 1: Prepare the manuscript
- Follow the journal's author guidelines and structure.
- Ensure reproducibility: include code, data, and full protocol in supplements.
- Write a clear cover letter (1–2 paragraphs) stating:
  - The core cognitive question you address.
  - Why this question matters.
  - How this work contributes.
  - Why the journal is a good fit.

### Step 2: Register or create an account
- **Cognitive Science:** Go to [Cognitive Science on Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/15516709), click "Submit an article," create an account, or log in.
- **eLife:** Go to [eLife submission portal](https://submit.elifesciences.org/), create or log in to your account.
- **Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience:** Go to [JCN on MIT Press](https://direct.mit.edu/jocn), click "Submit," register or log in.

### Step 3: Complete the submission form
- Upload manuscript, figures, and supplementary materials.
- Fill out author information (names, affiliations, roles, contact information).
- Provide a cover letter.
- Suggest 3–5 reviewers (researchers in computational cognitive science, connectomics, or strategic reasoning who are not collaborators or mentors).
- Declare conflicts of interest.
- Agree to ethical standards (manuscript is original, not under review elsewhere, etc.).

### Step 4: Track the submission
- After submission, you receive a confirmation email with a manuscript ID.
- Most journals allow you to log in and track status online.
- You may receive emails requesting clarifications or inviting revision.
- Respond promptly to all editor requests.

### Step 5: Peer review and revision
- Expect 6–12 weeks for peer review (sometimes longer).
- When the editor decision arrives, read the reviews carefully.
- If revisions are requested:
  - Write a detailed response letter addressing every reviewer comment.
  - Revise the manuscript, marking changes in track changes or a color-coded version.
  - Resubmit within the requested timeline (usually 4–8 weeks).
- If accepted, work with the journal on proofs and final formatting (2–4 weeks).

## Important notes

- **Rejection is possible.** Even well-designed experiments can receive desk rejections or negative peer reviews. Have a backup plan to revise and resubmit elsewhere.
- **Peer review is slow.** Budget 6–16 months for the full cycle, not weeks. Plan accordingly.
- **Reviewers will scrutinize claims.** They will ask: Is the result really about the connectome constraint? How much of the performance is search vs. evaluation? Does the rubric drive the result, or is it just a label? Have evidence and ablations ready.
- **Transparency is rewarded.** Journals like eLife and Cognitive Science value honest scope, clear methods, thorough ablations, and modest conclusions. This is where your project's careful design shines.
- **Reproducibility matters.** Release code and data (or a protocol for reproducibility) with the accepted paper. This increases impact and credibility.
