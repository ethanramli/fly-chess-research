## Verified entry facts

The official [rules](https://www.theearthprize.org/rules) allow secondary students worldwide born in 2007–2014, entering alone or in groups of up to five. Minors need parental consent. Registration closes **10 January 2027**. The [submission guidelines](https://www.theearthprize.org/participant-submission-package) set the project deadline at **31 January 2027, 12:00 CET**, which is **18:00 Jakarta**. No October 15 national deadline was found for this competition.

Register through the [official onboarding page](https://www.theearthprize.org/onboarding). Each teammate registers individually; the team lead creates the team and submits its project. An adult educator is encouraged. Registration has not been performed by this preparation.

## What to prepare

The first submission is a portal-based **Earth Prize Blueprint** with seven sections covering the problem, discovery, idea, creation, validation, implementation/funding and a video pitch. Real stakeholder engagement and something tangible to test underpin the proposal. Exact field limits and first-stage video duration must be checked in the portal; the approximately two-minute video specified for the later Scholar stage should not be assumed to apply at entry. [Submission guidance](https://www.theearthprize.org/participant-submission-package).

The [judging criteria](https://www.theearthprize.org/competition) allocate 25 points: problem validation and proof of concept receive 5 each; solution quality, next steps and feasibility receive 4 each; communication receives 3. Invest early effort in evidence and a testable prototype.

## Fit with FlyChess

The present FlyChess study asks about consequence-sensitive learning and transfer in a connectome-constrained computational model. Its inspected results do not establish an environmental outcome. Two planning routes remain open:

- Develop a separate environmental project with a stakeholder and a feasible local pilot.
- Explore a distinct application of the modeling work, but only after identifying an actual environmental decision, an appropriate comparison and measurable benefit.

Lower RAM usage does not establish lower energy use or emissions. A biological architecture does not by itself establish environmental usefulness. An efficiency application would need measured computation/energy at matched task quality, realistic deployment assumptions and a relevant environmental outcome. Preserve the existing neuroscience question and frozen experiments.

## Concept selection worksheet

Before writing a proposal, fill in these six sentences from your own observations:

1. **Problem:** [Which environmental problem can I observe and measure?]
2. **People:** [Who experiences it, and whom can I speak with?]
3. **Current approach:** [What already happens, and why does it fall short?]
4. **Intervention:** [What small change could I actually build and pilot?]
5. **Comparison:** [What baseline would make the test informative?]
6. **Impact:** [What direct outcome can I measure, over what period, and at what cost?]

A provisional example to investigate, not an evidence-backed chosen project: reduce avoidable canteen food waste by testing a simple pre-order or portion-planning process. First establish whether the canteen has this problem, agrees to a pilot, and can measure waste fairly. Use kilograms of avoidable waste per meal served and record menu/attendance changes. Technology choice follows the observed need; it does not need a neural model. No access, problem magnitude or effectiveness is assumed.

## Blueprint preparation worksheet

| Section | Work to do | Evidence to keep |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | Define a specific issue, location, affected group and environmental outcome. | Observations and reliable source records. |
| Discovery | Speak to affected people and understand constraints/current solutions. | Dated notes and consented, anonymised feedback. |
| Idea | Explain the proposed change and check alternatives. | Comparison with approaches already available. |
| Create | Build the smallest prototype that tests the central assumption. | Versioned artefact and test plan. |
| Validate | Compare against a baseline, collect feedback and revise. | Raw measurements, failures and changes. |
| Implementation/funding | Identify partners, concrete milestones, itemised costs and obstacles. | Quotes, permissions and a cost worksheet. |
| Pitch | Explain the problem, prototype, evidence and next step in your own voice. | A truthful video matched to portal instructions. |

Use citations for external facts. Make actual team contributions clear and retain your own observations, decisions, failures and evidence; check the current portal instructions on assistance before submitting. Earth Prize rules are separate from the OPSI guidance previously researched.
