# Manuscript outline

Working article type: empirical computational cognitive-modeling paper.

## Working title

Strategic evaluation and generalization in a connectome-constrained chess model

## Abstract

State the cognitive question, why chess is an appropriate testbed, the matched model/training conditions, held-out transfer measures, main results, and the narrow conclusion supported by the evidence. Do not equate a playing rating with human-like cognition.

## Introduction

- Explain why strategic evaluation and generalization matter to cognitive science.
- Review what is known about chess cognition, learned evaluation, search, and biologically inspired/connectome-constrained models.
- Identify the uncertainty: whether explicit interpretable assessment can support transfer under architectural constraints.
- State the primary question and preregistered or protocol-defined hypotheses.
- Explain why the model is a computational testbed, not a claim about biological flies.

## Methods

- Connectome, simulator, input encoding, readout, trainable parameters, and engineered components.
- Position-assessment rubric and label provenance.
- Data sources, licenses, leakage-safe splits, and held-out novel-motif tests.
- Matched training conditions and search ablations.
- Outcomes: transfer, criterion-level evaluation, tactical errors, playing strength, compute, memory, and latency.
- Seeds, stopping rules, analysis, ethics/access, and reproducibility materials.

## Results / findings

Report all planned outcomes, including null and unexpected findings. Separate evaluator performance from model-plus-search performance. Show whether rubric supervision improves transfer and whether the effect survives matched controls.

## Discussion

- Interpret findings in relation to strategic cognition and prior computational models.
- State what the result does and does not establish.
- Discuss the roles of evaluation, search, memory, architecture, and supervision.
- Address alternative explanations, limitations, and reproducibility.
- Place website, online learning, explanation, rating, and pondering features in future work unless directly tested.

## Declarations and supporting material

Funding; conflicts; author contributions; ethics and permissions; data, code, model, configuration, and checkpoint availability; compute statement; references; supplementary analyses; and the complete protocol/amendment history.
