# Manuscript outline

Working article type: empirical computational cognitive-modeling paper.

## Working title

Experience-dependent plasticity and transferable reasoning in a fly-connectome-constrained chess model

## Abstract

Describe whether a simple organism-derived computational model can acquire reasoning. Explain why chess tests transfer of consequence-sensitive evaluation, then summarize the learning mechanism, matched conditions, held-out measures, and conclusion supported by the evidence. Distinguish model capability from cognition in a living fly, and chess transfer from reasoning across domains. Report playing strength separately.

## Introduction

- Explain the overarching question: can a fly-connectome-constrained model acquire transferable reasoning from experience?
- Review what is known about chess cognition, learned evaluation, search, and biologically inspired/connectome-constrained models.
- Identify the uncertainty: whether biologically motivated experience-dependent plasticity supports consequence-sensitive evaluation and transfer under the stated architectural constraints.
- State the primary question and preregistered or protocol-defined hypotheses.
- Explain why the model is a computational testbed, not a claim about biological flies.

## Methods

- Connectome, simulator, input encoding, readout, trainable parameters, and engineered components.
- Describe verified experiences and consequences, any engine-assisted value condition, signal limits, and evidence sources. Distinguish these from direct criterion supervision.
- Data sources, licenses, leakage-safe splits, and held-out unfamiliar/counterfactual position families.
- Matched training conditions and search ablations.
- Report causal learning, retention and reversal, consequence-sensitive transfer, unaided choices, choices with a fixed search budget, calibrated playing strength, compute, memory, and latency.
- Seeds, stopping rules, analysis, ethics/access, and reproducibility materials.

## Results / findings

Report planned outcomes, including null and unexpected findings. Separate neural evaluation and unaided choices from model-plus-search performance. Test whether informative experience changes behavior causally and transfers to positions outside the training families under matched controls.

## Discussion

- Interpret findings in relation to strategic cognition and prior computational models.
- State what the result does and does not establish.
- Discuss the roles and limits of evaluation, search, memory, architecture and verified experience-dependent plasticity.
- Address alternative explanations, limitations, and reproducibility.
- Place website, online learning, explanation, rating, and pondering features in future work unless directly tested.

## Declarations and supporting material

Funding; conflicts; author contributions; ethics and permissions; data, code, model, configuration, and checkpoint availability; compute statement; references; supplementary analyses; and the complete protocol/amendment history.
