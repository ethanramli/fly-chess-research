# Delta-focused candidate generation

The full-legal ranking used in the adaptive feedback experiment is a diagnostic baseline. It is expensive and deliberately exposes candidate omission: if the correct move is absent from a shortlist, a ranking improvement cannot repair it. It is not the intended long-term puzzle policy.

After the opponent moves, a faster policy can compute a public board-state delta: changed squares, captures, arrivals, check status and changed attack relationships. The current candidate generator tags checks, captures/recaptures, moves touching changed squares, and newly created attacks on enemy pieces (including discovered attacks and double attacks). It orders checks first, then captures weighted coarsely by victim value, new attacks weighted by target value, and moves touching the delta. It reserves a small number of quiet legal actions. This is an engineering priority score, not a chess evaluation: it does not establish that a capture is sound, an attacked piece is trapped, or a move is best. Search and independent consequence verification must test the opponent's replies.

`.codebase/src/search-and-feedback/delta-forcing-candidates.mjs` implements the deterministic construction. It reports the total legal count and omission count, so candidate omission stays measurable. Capture-square extraction excludes a merely vacated origin; this matters when tagging a later recapture. Geometric attack detection is not natural visual perception and can include attacks that are tactically unsound. The module does not apply neural updates or use teacher labels. The symbolic encoder remains a separate interface.

The required comparison is paired evaluation: run the delta shortlist for speed, then run the all-legal diagnostic with the same memory snapshot, puzzle and search budget. Record whether the independently verified successful move was generated, how many candidates were omitted, wall/CPU time, and whether the shortlist changes the neural choice. A shortcut that is faster but omits the correct move is a search failure, not reasoning. The selector is available as opt-in PUCT candidate generation; it remains experimental until multi-position, counterfactual and fixed-search-budget tests characterize omission and choice quality.

The current module tests cover changed-square extraction, check/capture priority, direct/discovered/double-attack tags, quiet reservation and explicit omission counts. A warmed software microcheck on one 32-legal-move position produced 16 candidates in a 9.80 ms median over 50 calls. A paired, repeated full-graph **standalone assessment** then compared 32 legal candidates against 16 shortlist candidates, with four quiet slots and no position-delta predecessor. The all-legal policy and shortlist both selected `c4b4`, independently verified as failing the task. The shortlist retained `c4g8`, the fixture's sole independently audited successful move. Its score was 0.013221 versus 0.020617 for `c4b4`, leaving a 0.007396 ranking gap. Mean assessment time fell from 22.08 s to 12.47 s (43.5%) on this one position. This profile is distinct from actual PUCT tree search below.

The generator is now an opt-in `candidateMode: 'forcing'` in `monte-carlo-puzzle-search.mjs`; default `all-legal` behavior remains unchanged. At each expanded position, checks, captures, newly created attacks and a quiet reserve determine which actions receive neural evaluations. The search reports bounded per-depth candidate/omission counts and root tags. Across two separate runs per mode under the same two-evaluation budget, forcing mode generated 32 candidate cues per search versus 48 for all-legal, retained root `c4g8`, and reduced median CPU time from 17.44 s to 13.70 s. Median elapsed time was 25.54 s versus 32.11 s. All four runs chose `c4b4`, independently verified as failing the three-ply mate objective. This preliminary one-position efficiency result changed neither decision quality nor learning and is sensitive to host timing variation. See the [comparison report](../../../archive/early-development/research/reports/forcing-monte-carlo-v3-2026-10-07/README.md) and [individual forcing report](../../../archive/early-development/research/reports/forcing-monte-carlo-v1-2026-10-07/README.md).

The held-out construction probe [`evaluate-delta-heldout.mjs`](../../../.codebase/scripts/evaluation-and-performance/evaluate-delta-heldout.mjs) uses the `coverage-pilot-v2` test families without fitting or selecting on them. It reconstructs a one-move parent-to-child transition where the FEN permits exact matching, then measures the shortlist only. On 55 source rows (110 child states), 91 transitions resolved and 19 were explicitly unresolved because the recorded parent/child FEN was not a direct one-move match. Resolved states averaged 31.99 legal moves and 15.46 selected candidates, omitting 1,504 legal candidates in total. This is a construction/latency diagnostic with no best-move labels, neural inference, feedback or transfer claim; unresolved transitions must not be silently treated as failures.

[`paired-candidate-assessment.mjs`](../../../.codebase/src/search-and-feedback/paired-candidate-assessment.mjs) evaluates all legal moves and the delta shortlist under the same read-only memory fingerprint, returning choice agreement and omitted move identities. Its fixture test confirms two assessments, unchanged state and explicit omissions. The current full suite has passed 98/98 after opt-in PUCT integration. No neural decision improvement is claimed for the revised generator.

The first bounded full-graph probe used two resolved held-out states with the original encoder and no learning. Mean legal count was 29.5 and shortlist count 14. Choices agreed on one state. On the other, the all-legal choice `g7g6` was omitted and the shortlist chose `a7a5`; this is a measured candidate-omission failure, not evidence that either move is correct. The probe took 10.42 s and 15.80 s for the two states. Raw report: `research/reports/paired-delta-full-graph-v1-2026-10-06/report.json`.

A controlled widening probe used the same two states and frozen model, with 24 candidates and eight quiet slots. Choices agreed 2/2, including the previously omitted `g7g6`; mean selected count rose to 24 from 29.5 legal moves and total omission fell to 11. This is a candidate-coverage improvement, not correctness or transfer: the held-out rows have no independently verified best-move labels. Raw report: `research/reports/paired-delta-full-graph-wide-v1-2026-10-06/report.json`. Both full-graph probes predate the new attack tags and priority ordering above; they do not validate this updated generator.

The first correctness probe used the two declared puzzle fixtures with the same 24/8 shortlist and independently verified the focused choice. KRK selected `a1b1`, verified successful; KQK selected `f7f5`, verified failure. All-legal and focused choices agreed on both positions, so the KQK failure is a neural ranking failure after candidate generation, not an omission. Raw report: `research/reports/paired-delta-fixture-correctness-v1-2026-10-06/report.json`. This is the useful baseline for teaching: the correct legal move is available, but feedback must change its ranking.
