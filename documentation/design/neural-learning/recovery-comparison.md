# Local recovery experiment: matched exposure comparison

2026-10-05. Frozen [protocol](../../../research/data/recovery-comparison-v1/protocol.json) before the first run. This tests a source-derived computational candidate on the released graph; it does not validate biological physiology, GF choice, chess learning or full-model adaptation. Original gamma4 V1/V2 failures remain unchanged.

The [mapped recovery candidate](reversal-mechanism.md) can depress active-cue efficacy and recover inactive-cue efficacy under identified DAN modulation. The prior gamma4 experiment lacked that latter rule. These models also differ in cells, cue pools, presentation timing and readout, so their outcomes cannot isolate recovery as the sole cause of any difference.

Six new cue seeds (7001, 7013, 7027, 7043, 7057, 7079), four conditions, four acquisition presentations and twelve reversal presentations give 384 bounded training events. Cues are two disjoint 5% samples of eligible KC identities; they alternate in target-blind order. Initial punished identity counterbalances across seeds and reverses after acquisition. Balanced presentations control exposure rather than testing autonomous exploration or behavior-contingent action learning.

The teacher supplies cue presentations and a fixed direct stimulation pulse to the two mapped PPL103 cells when appropriate. It supplies no parameter deltas. The candidate reads measured excess KC and mapped PPL103/PAM02 endpoint activity, then changes only its 870 allowed efficacy gains. Recurrent DAN responses can cause changes without external feedback; the absent condition measures this drift rather than requiring it to remain zero. The stimulation is an experimental interface, not a reconstructed shock pathway.

Conditions: informative punishment, no external pulse, phase-wise pulse-count-matched shuffled stimulation, and informative stimulation with plasticity disabled. Every condition has identical cue identities and presentation order within a seed. The shuffled sequence may have accidental informative pairings; its exact permutation and counts are audited. Disabled state and probes must remain unchanged. There is no optimizer, externally calculated negative update, updated encoder or trained head.

Fixed engineered score is mean activity of the four MBON12 cells minus mean activity of the two MBON01 cells, favoring the higher score. The decoder receives responses alone. Probes stimulate cues without updating memory; they do not supply labels. Baseline, acquisition, repeated-probe persistence, reversal, naive reset and learned-state restoration are recorded. Persistence is stored-state consistency without an elapsed biological-time model.

Frozen gates require every seed to choose the safe cue after acquisition and reversal with positive target-signed effects. Reversal change must exceed absent and shuffled controls under exact one-sided sign tests at alpha 0.05. Exact reset/restoration, persistence, disabled controls, matched counts and fixed wiring/nonplastic parameters are also required. Every gate must pass; positive response movement alone is insufficient. Neither the decoder nor gates are tuned after observing results.

The independent audit of the complete original run checks all 384 local-update transitions; a resumed invocation alone lacks its prefix's checkpoint trace and is not a substitute for the full audit. A two-trial prefix replay across the acquisition/reversal boundary is a separate resumability check, not proof of every possible interruption or a full replay.

Interpretation must retain the remaining requirements: coarse subtype masks lack synapse-coordinate/receptor validation; restart-per-presentation dynamics differ from continuous physiology; negative modulation polarity is a source-derived model assumption; the software score is not an animal action or GF readout; independent biological predictions remain untested. Success here can support further candidate investigations but cannot establish consequence-based chess reasoning or useful transfer.
