## Frozen experiment

Eight public four-bit target cues each offer actions A/B. The independently reproducible contingency table supplies a favorable and adverse outcome per cue. Acquisition presents both actions twice: 32 known experiences. Four separate public cues supply 16 intervening teaching experiences; reassess the original target associations afterward. Reversal supplies 32 experiences with target contingencies inverted. Reversal changes synthetic task truth, not legal chess consequences.

Every experience records the unaided two-action neural ranking before feedback and rechecks its table certificate. Public cue/action is the entire encoder input; phase, criterion labels and certificates stay teacher-side. Unknown produces no teaching frames. Informative, absent, phase/action-matched shuffled and disabled arms receive identical public exposure/order/timing. Shuffle assignments are frozen before informative execution. Absent removes external pulses only; disabled holds all gains and traces fixed while receiving informative pulses.

Explicit approximations remain: coarse compartment map, unavailable synapse-coordinate/receptor identities, fixed-reference normalization, frame-held gains, numerical time and engineered pulse-order teaching. These are not natural reward, biological clock/retention or reconstructed receptor kinetics.

Three predetermined projection seeds must actually evoke different MBON responses; unused output-head seeds do not count. These effective-replica measurements occur inside the feedback-enabled study. ## Acceptance and stopping

In each replica, after every phase require at least 6/8 correct target preferences, at least two additional correct contexts over each matched control, and positive mean correctly oriented preference exceeding each control by epsilon1e-8. Ties are incorrect; every failed context remains reported. Interference retention tests the original acquisition contingency; reversal tests its inverted counterpart. No advancement on margins alone.

Run complete matched phases in order, first replica first. A failed required phase stops later phases/replicas before further exposure. Unrun phases remain unknown/unpassed. This prospective stopping rule implements the curriculum's failure-stops-advancement requirement; it neither weakens completed gates nor claims later failures/successes. Successful G0 requires all three phases in all three effective replicas plus causal-state/recovery/replay checks. G0 alone would not establish disjoint chess transfer or reasoning.

At phase endpoints verify unchanged fixed state, remove learned physiological state and lose the reference-relative effect, then restore exactly. Keep each arm's state continuous into later phases; assessment/reset interventions are restored before subsequent teaching. Stream all raw receipts and separately replay certificates, rankings, every neural frame/local state, all matched-arm lesson counts, phase gates and reset/restoration from archived source bytes. The auditor shares the original neural dynamics and plasticity integrator; it is computational replay, not independent biological validation or an external-agent review.
