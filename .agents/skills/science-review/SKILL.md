# Science review

Find the strongest claim the evidence supports and the smallest check that could change it. Use this on its own for a scoped review. In [Problem Solving](../problem-solving/SKILL.md), use it to sharpen the existing reviewers' briefs. The lead owns iteration and adjudicates objections against evidence. Use [Deep Research](../deep-research/SKILL.md) when a consequential claim depends on outside evidence.

## Frame the decision

Identify the proposal revision, the decision it informs, the exact claim, the system and conditions it covers, and the evidence needed to support it. For FlyChess, name the volume and chapter and follow the [project's research rules](../../../AGENTS.md). Start from the pinned protocol and relevant run records, analysis, audit or proof. State which records you inspected and which decisive records are missing. Use [the review guide](review-guide.md) for checks that fit the claim.

## Try to break the claim

1. **Trace the claim.** Locate the decisive observation or proof. Check the exact comparison, outcome, denominator or unit, uncertainty, deviations from the plan, and what the audit actually covered. Assess how much the finding matters and how strongly the evidence supports it as separate questions.
2. **Name the strongest rival or counterexample.** Write what the proposed explanation and the rival predict. For a model, check which component supplied the behavior. For a proof, ask which assumption lets the result hold and whether a case outside it could overturn a broader claim.
3. **Choose a check that changes the decision.** Specify the smallest check, what each plausible outcome would imply, and which stage to reopen. Use already authorized evidence or checks. For a protocol, check whether the design could yield interpretable positive and negative results. For results, distinguish support, evidence against a stated effect within the test's sensitivity, and an inconclusive test. A failed prerequisite, uncontrolled rival, missing artifact, or interval that still allows a meaningful effect can leave the downstream claim open.
4. **Return a review the lead can act on.** Give the strongest supported sentence, the decision it permits, and the material objections in priority order. For each objection give the revision and challenged claim, evidence locator or missing artifact, consequence if true, smallest check and how to interpret it, and the step to reopen. Keep missing evidence and reviewer dissent visible. Say what you checked when no material objection survives.

For a revised proposal, recheck materially changed claims and the earlier objections they affect. Keep prior reviews only where the claim, method, and evidence dependencies still match. Preserve frozen criteria and label outcome-informed analyses as exploratory. Follow project rules for recording mistakes and pivots. A review informs a research decision and carries its own evidence scope. Execution and independent run audits follow their existing rules.
