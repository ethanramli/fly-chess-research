# Science review

Find the strongest claim the evidence supports and the smallest check that could change it. Use this on its own for a scoped review. In [Problem Solving](../problem-solving/SKILL.md), use it to sharpen the existing reviewers' briefs. The lead owns iteration and adjudicates objections against evidence. Open [Deep Research](../deep-research/SKILL.md) only when outside evidence could change the review and outside research is in scope.

## Decide when to review

Choose scope before analyzing the evidence. A request to review the **first evidence for the main claim** is a full project review, even when the question is phrased as “can we say this yet?” A major change to the main question or design, a submission review, or a requested overall score also triggers a full review. Respect an explicit instruction to review only a named part or give only a qualitative judgment.

Use the [ISEF Grand Award rubric and scoring worksheet](references/isef-grand-award-rubric.md). Choose the science or engineering track by what the main contribution is meant to establish. If the worksheet is unavailable within the permitted sources, assess the evidence qualitatively and mark the numerical score unavailable.

| When | Review |
| --- | --- |
| Before committing to a decisive protocol, measure, control, or prototype test | Focus on the design and the criteria the decision could affect. Give the points at risk and the check that would settle the issue. |
| After a result or revision could change a substantive conclusion | Recheck the affected rows and their dependencies. In Heavy or deep-in-thought, use the existing reviewer passes and update the affected findings each round. |
| When the first evidence for the main claim is ready, after a major change to the main question or design, before submission, or on explicit request for an overall score | Run the full review. Score the whole project and explain every point lost. Reopen any material flaw through the [review loop](../problem-solving/SKILL.md#run-the-review-loop). |
| Routine implementation, copy edits, or reruns that leave the evidence and decision unchanged | Keep working without a rubric review. |

After a full review, carry forward unchanged evidence and rescore affected criteria on each material revision. Keep a fixed time schedule only if the researcher asks for one.

## Frame the decision

Identify the proposal revision, the decision it informs, the exact claim, the system and conditions it covers, and the evidence needed to support it. For FlyChess, name the volume and chapter and follow the [project's research rules](../../../AGENTS.md). Start from the pinned protocol and relevant run records, analysis, audit or proof. State which records you inspected and which decisive records are missing. Open [the review guide](review-guide.md) for a claim-specific check when the basic trace leaves a material question.

## Try to break the claim

1. **Trace the claim.** Locate the decisive observation or proof. Check the exact comparison, outcome, denominator or unit, uncertainty, deviations from the plan, and what the audit actually covered. Assess how much the finding matters and how strongly the evidence supports it as separate questions.
2. **Name the strongest rival or counterexample.** Write what the proposed explanation and the rival predict. For a model, check which component supplied the behavior. For a proof, ask which assumption lets the result hold and whether a case outside it could overturn a broader claim.
3. **Choose a check that changes the decision.** Specify the smallest check, what each plausible outcome would imply, and which stage to reopen. Use already authorized evidence or checks. For a protocol, check whether the design could yield interpretable positive and negative results. For results, distinguish support, evidence against a stated effect within the test's sensitivity, and an inconclusive test. A failed prerequisite, uncontrolled rival, missing artifact, or interval that still allows a meaningful effect can leave the downstream claim open.
4. **Return a review the lead can act on.** Give the strongest supported sentence, the decision it permits, and the material objections in priority order. For each objection give the revision and challenged claim, evidence locator or missing artifact, consequence if true, smallest check and how to interpret it, and the step to reopen. Keep missing evidence and reviewer dissent visible. Say what you checked when no material objection survives.

For a revised proposal, recheck materially changed claims and the earlier objections they affect. Keep prior reviews only where the claim, method, and evidence dependencies still match. Preserve frozen criteria and label outcome-informed analyses as exploratory. Follow project rules for recording mistakes and pivots. A review informs a research decision and carries its own evidence scope. Execution and independent run audits follow their existing rules.

## Report the score and next decision

Lead with what the evidence lets the reader say and which finding drives the next decision. Name the reviewed revision, track, and evidence available.

- **Focused review.** Name each affected worksheet row and its point exposure or scored deduction, with the exact evidence, why it matters, and the check that would change the finding. Label the row maximum as a ceiling when the rest of the row was outside scope.
- **Full review.** Score each worksheet row once using a single ledger. Score the engineering Success criteria row on the clarity and justification of the stated thresholds, load, and conditions. If you deduct there, name what is missing from the definition or rationale. Charge missing measurements, untested conditions, or a result below threshold to execution only. Mark evidence that was never supplied as unavailable, and use zero for interview rows when there are no actual answers. An outline can support only the part of display organization it actually shows.
- **Publish from the ledger.** Follow the [full scorecard instructions](references/isef-grand-award-rubric.md#generate-a-full-scorecard). Include the generated row table and totals in the requested scorecard. Use those tables as the only source for numerical totals. Fix validation errors before publishing. If you cannot verify a score in the available environment, give the qualitative review and say what blocks the score.
- **Ask what would change the answer.** Keep a failed requirement or unsupported central claim open regardless of the score. Give the smallest in-scope check and say how its possible results would change the decision. For a paper, evaluate any venue-specific requirements separately when relevant.
