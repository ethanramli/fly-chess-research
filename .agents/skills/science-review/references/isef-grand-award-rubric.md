# ISEF Grand Award rubric

Source: [Society for Science, Regeneron ISEF Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/). Checked 2026-11-13. The tables paraphrase the judging criteria and preserve the published section weights.

| Section | Science project | Engineering project | Maximum |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| Question or problem | Focused, testable question and contribution to the field | Practical need, success criteria, and constraints | 10 |
| Design and methodology | Study design and data collection, with appropriate variables and controls | Alternatives considered, solution selected, and prototype or model developed | 15 |
| Execution | Systematic collection and analysis, reproducibility, suitable mathematics or statistics, and sufficient evidence | Prototype demonstrates the intended design, has been tested under multiple conditions or trials, and shows engineering skill and completeness | 20 |
| Creativity and potential impact | Originality and actual or plausible impact | Originality and actual or plausible impact | 20 |
| Presentation | Poster or display (10) and interview (25) | Poster or display (10) and interview (25) | 35 |
| **Total** | | | **100** |

The display criteria cover organization, understandable figures, and supporting records. The interview covers clear answers, command of the science, interpretation and limitations, researcher independence, impact, and next research ideas. Assess each member's contribution and understanding for team projects. For a continuation, identify the current year's work and use it for the interview assessment. Use the notebook and Forms 1C and 7 when applicable. The official page gives suggested section scores and leaves the individual items unweighted.

## Strict scoring worksheet

The allocations below are this skill's internal way of accounting for every point. Use one track per score. Choose by the main contribution. Science tests an explanation or establishes a finding. Engineering solves a practical problem under constraints. For mixed projects, score the primary contribution, explain the choice, and use the other track as a secondary check or a separately labeled score when requested.

| Science criterion | Points | Evidence for full credit |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Focus and purpose | 3 | State the exact question, system, and scope in terms that define what will be tested. |
| Contribution to the field | 3 | Identify a real gap against the closest relevant prior work and explain what closing it would teach. |
| Testability | 4 | Link the question to a measurable outcome, rival predictions where applicable, and a decision rule. |
| Study and collection design | 7 | Show a workable plan with justified sampling or case selection, measurements, and analysis and interpretation rules appropriate to the field. |
| Variables and controls | 8 | Show that the design isolates what drives the result, with credible controls for the strongest rivals and leakage where relevant. |
| Systematic collection and analysis | 5 | Trace evidence through collection, exclusions, processing, and analysis. Document deviations. |
| Reproducibility | 5 | Show enough material, data, analysis and records for a qualified reader to repeat or independently verify the decisive work. |
| Mathematics and statistics | 5 | Justify the methods, units, assumptions, and uncertainty, or supply a rigorous derivation and its domain. |
| Sufficiency for the conclusion | 5 | Show the result can distinguish the claimed outcomes at the stated scope and sensitivity. Include applicable prerequisites and limitations. |

| Engineering criterion | Points | Evidence for full credit |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Practical need | 3 | Show the intended users or use case and evidence that the problem matters. |
| Success criteria | 4 | Define measurable, justified requirements and pass thresholds. |
| Constraints | 3 | Identify the real limits on the design and the relevant tradeoffs. |
| Alternatives | 5 | Compare credible alternatives against comparable criteria and constraints. |

## Award points and document deductions

- Start each row at zero. Award only whole points earned by the supplied, verified evidence. Use **0** when evidence is absent, unusable, or fails the criterion. Label unavailable, pending, and observed failures separately. Give at most half the points, rounded down, for preliminary evidence that leaves a central part unestablished. A plan earns design credit where it meets the design criteria. Give execution credit for evidence already produced and relevant to the row. Reserve the upper range below full credit for strong evidence with specific gaps. Full credit requires evidence for the entire row at the scale of the claim and the strongest relevant challenge addressed. Apply the same anchors to repeated reviews.
- For each scored row record **earned / available**, **points lost**, the exact evidence locator or missing artifact, and what would earn the lost points. Explain the amount. Group points only when the same reason and fix cover them. Track linked gaps across criteria and deduct again only if a separate requirement is unmet. For instance, a clear research gap can earn question credit while the method still lacks an original contribution. For a full review, use the ledger and generator below to calculate earned and lost points. Example: 2/5 for reproducibility, 3 lost. The raw observations and analysis steps are available, but a reader cannot reconstruct the inputs or repeat the decisive comparison. Earn two of those points with the versioned input manifest and the last point with a successful repeat or independent verification.
- For a **full project review**, score all rows. Show the research subtotal out of 65 and, for ISEF preparation, the display and interview scores out of 10 and 25. If either artifact or interview evidence is missing, its points remain unearned and the total out of 100 is provisional. Report why. Use actual answers from the researcher or a supplied interview record. Proposed talking points can prepare the researcher but cannot earn interview credit.
- For a **focused review**, identify the affected rows and show the deductions or points at risk for those rows only. A planned test can earn credit for a supported design, while its execution evidence remains pending. Carry forward existing scores only when the evidence and claim are unchanged. For journal or other research reviews, report the research score out of 65 and apply venue-specific communication requirements separately when relevant. If asked to simulate ISEF judging, report the full scorecard.
- Name the strongest supported conclusion, the largest fixable deductions, and the weakest link in the main claim. Compare novelty and evidence against the closest credible field work. For a full ISEF readiness review, compare the work with relevant Grand Award projects when accessible. Prefer similar problems, evidence, and methods. Use public abstracts to compare disclosed scope and contribution. Award outcomes alone cannot establish how a judge scored a project. These are internal evidence judgments. The published criteria provide no winning cutoff. A strong total cannot settle a central problem with inference, verification, or a prototype's core requirements.

## Generate a full scorecard

Use JSON with one `track` (`science` or `engineering`) and a `rows` array. Set `artifacts` to three booleans: `display_graphics`, `display_evidence`, and `interview`. Mark each true only when the corresponding figures, records as displayed, or actual interview answers were inspected. Every row carries the exact worksheet `criterion` name, whole `earned` points, `status` (`observed`, `pending`, or `unavailable`), and `evidence` (locator and reason, or the missing artifact). For a row with lost points, give `next` with the action that could earn them. Use observed for results actually supported by a record, including results that fail. Pending or unavailable rows earn zero.

Pass the ledger through stdin to the [scorecard generator](../scripts/render-scorecard.py). Run it with `python -B .agents/skills/science-review/scripts/render-scorecard.py` from the repository root. Use a heredoc if the task does not authorize writing a ledger file. The generator reads the worksheet, validates every row and section, and prints the Markdown headline and tables together. Check that the cited evidence supports the assigned points before using its output. When Python is unavailable, reconcile the same ledger by summing the earned and lost columns independently and confirming all section denominators. Publish a number only if both sums can be verified.
