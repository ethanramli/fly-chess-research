## 1. What the dry run shows

**The dense-KC hypothesis is supported as a description of this model’s recorded activity, but not yet proved as the cause of the behavioral failure.** The trace reports every mapped KC active, weak differences between practised cues, near-equal legal-move scores, and a nearly common score shift after learning ([journal, lines 141–144](</C:/Users/ethan/Documents/fly-chess-research/research/journal/experiments/2026-11-07-fly-connections-memory.md:141>)). Those observations fit the hypothesis that the activity-gated update writes broadly without separating moves. Under the implemented rule, when gain starts at 1, the first change is proportional to DAN factor times KC activity ([rule, lines 78–96](</C:/Users/ethan/Documents/fly-chess-research/.codebase/src/neural-learning/reversal-memory.mjs:78>)).

Ranked explanations:

1. **Weak within-board KC separation plus broadly positive activity.** This is the strongest explanation: the recorded cue patterns differ a little, but all KCs contribute positive activity to the update. The observed 1,809 mask edges with at least 1% cumulative change show that weights move; the absence of B-phase pressure writes on A-written edges and zero A/B acquisition show that broad change did not produce the desired behavior ([journal](</C:/Users/ethan/Documents/fly-chess-research/research/journal/experiments/2026-11-07-fly-connections-memory.md:141>)).
2. **The MBON05 score may have little move-specific sensitivity to those gains.** Scores are the negative mean of two MBON05 rates; other network inputs can contribute to those rates, and the code does not decompose KC-driven from non-KC-driven output ([condition, lines 235–245](</C:/Users/ethan/Documents/fly-chess-research/.codebase/src/neural-learning/fly-connections-condition-v1.mjs:235>)). Non-KC dominance is plausible, not established.
3. **APL and encoding may leave the KC population too tonic.** The local APL adapter replaces KC currents with a spatially filtered APL field, but has no explicit KC active-set threshold ([APL, lines 75–89](</C:/Users/ethan/Documents/fly-chess-research/.codebase/src/neural-learning/fly-connections-apl-v1.mjs:75>)). The tests show the adapter follows its reference implementation; they do not show that its production field produces biologically sparse chess responses.
4. **Eight steps and the small teaching budget may limit acquisition.** They are possible contributors, but the full run keeps the same eight-step forward pass. More boards alone will not fix an uninformative representation or readout.
5. **Tie tolerance is not the explanation.** The ranking tie tolerance is about \(3\times10^{-9}\) at scores near −0.023 ([metrics, lines 5–8 and 45–47](</C:/Users/ethan/Documents/fly-chess-research/.codebase/src/neural-learning/fly-connections-metrics-v1.mjs:5>)). The reported score spread is tens of thousands of times larger. Scores are close, but they are not being collapsed into ties by the tolerance.

The input is not wholly absent from the mapped KCs: the recorded patterns differ across cues. That does **not** establish a biologically faithful visual route. Project scope says the symbolic chess encoders bypass modeled eyes; natural vision is unimplemented.

The absent arm still runs endogenous updates; off skips them. The requested tests verify these distinctions.

Test games are separate from practice games. The clues are broadly honest, but the opening list is not a complete stand-alone scope statement: it should also foreground the one-ply capture criterion and the task-selected test set; the latter is disclosed later in gates section 4b.3.

**There is one red criterion in the teaching-causality gate.** The judge compares informative and controls on pooled A+B success, while its own-baseline check is split by group ([judge, lines 218–237](</C:/Users/ethan/Documents/fly-chess-research/.codebase/src/neural-learning/fly-connections-judge-v1.mjs:218>)). A strong effect in A could conceal a failure to beat controls in B, or vice versa. That can pass while group-specific teaching dependence fails. Revision 2 requires the A/B learning claim; make the control comparisons separate for A and B in the gates, judge, auditor, and counterexample tests before confirmation. The current dry run fails regardless, so this does not change its reported verdict.

The other main gates match the stated margins: A retention at both post-B and final, B acquisition against its own post-R1 baseline, effective-write pressure, R correction, and new-game comparisons against off and shuffled ([gates, lines 35–53](</C:/Users/ethan/Documents/fly-chess-research/documentation/design/neural-learning/fly-connections-memory-v1-gates-2026-11-08.md:35>); [retention judge, lines 85–123](</C:/Users/ethan/Documents/fly-chess-research/.codebase/src/neural-learning/fly-connections-metrics-v1.mjs:85>)).

## 3. Source-supported redesign options

- **Calibrate existing KC/APL dynamics:** Honegger et al. report sparse odor-evoked KC responses, and Lin et al. show APL feedback helps sparsify and decorrelate odor responses ([Honegger et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3180869/); [Lin et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4000970/)). This supports sparse coding as a biological design target, **not** a 5–10% target for this symbolic chess input or visual gamma-d KCs. A fixed, predeclared adjustment to modeled APL/KC dynamics could leave learning confined to the registered mask, but an outcome-tuned threshold or top-k filter would be an unsupported extra computation and would need a new registration.
- **Subtract a resting activity vector:** this is not directly supported by the cited rule. In Gkanias et al., \(w_\text{rest}=1\) is the **resting synaptic weight**, not a resting firing-rate baseline ([Gkanias et al., equations 18–20](https://elifesciences.org/articles/75611)). `ReversalMemory.observe(rest)` subtracts a neuronal-rate vector; revision 2 expressly uses zero and rejects counterfactual cue/DAN subtraction ([revision 2, lines 7–15](</C:/Users/ethan/Documents/fly-chess-research/documentation/design/neural-learning/fly-connections-memory-v1-revision-2-2026-11-07.md:7>)). A blank-trial baseline would therefore change the registered rule and should not be slipped in as a parameter tweak.
- **Restrict to visual gamma-d KCs:** Vogt et al. support a visual role for gamma-d KCs and visual responses in some of them ([Vogt et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4884080/)). Anatomical restriction is not extra-circuit arithmetic if fixed before training, but it only helps if the current encoder actually drives this visual pathway and the selected KCs have the relevant MBON05/PAM07 connections. It narrows the claim; it does not itself fix sparse coding or score sensitivity.

None of these sources validates a specific numerical correction for the present model. Choose and justify a mechanism before a new confirmatory design; do not tune against this dry-run outcome and then call the same run confirmatory.

The dry run is useful Volume 2 development evidence that this implementation engages plasticity but did not acquire or transfer the criterion on its small sample. It is not confirmatory evidence. It does not prove a Volume 4 limit. A limit claim needs a bound over the declared state/input/training domain on the **change in pairwise score differences**, e.g.  
\(\sup_{t,i,j}|(s_i-s_j)_t-(s_i-s_j)_0|\),  
and a comparison to the smallest initial ranking margin. The observed nearly common shift on these boards is only a finite empirical measurement, not such a bound.
