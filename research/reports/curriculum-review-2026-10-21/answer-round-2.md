**3.3. Mathematical Analysis of the Bidirectional Plasticity Rule and Pulse-Order Code (Questions 2.4 & 2.5):**
Let us integrate your exact differential equations over the $19$-frame replay episode to see why G0 reversal exhibits extreme margin attenuation ($\approx 10^{-4}$).
* **Time constants and frame discretization:** Each frame has $8$ recurrent steps of $\Delta t = 0.075$ model units, so **1 frame $= 8 \times 0.075 = 0.60$ model time units**, and a $19$-frame episode spans $T_{\text{ep}} = 11.4$ model time units. Compare this to the filter time constants:
  $$\tau_{\text{KC}} = 0.4, \quad \tau_{\text{Short}} = 0.6, \quad \tau_{\text{Long}} = 1.0$$
  Notice that $\tau_{\text{KC}} = 0.4$ is **shorter than a single frame ($0.60$)**!
* **Analytical trace dynamics under the two pulse orders:**
  For a step input of DAN activity $r > 0$ during a pulse frame, $S(t)$ rises faster than $L(t)$ because $\tau_{\text{Short}} = 0.6 < \tau_{\text{Long}} = 1.0$. Thus:
  * **During the DAN pulse onset:** $S(t) > L(t) \implies \delta(t) = L(t) - S(t) < 0$ (**Depression phase**, pushing $g \to g_{\min} = 0.2$).
  * **After the DAN pulse ends ($r \to 0$):** $S(t)$ decays faster than $L(t)$ ($e^{-t/0.6} < e^{-t/1.0}$), so $L(t) > S(t) \implies \delta(t) = L(t) - S(t) > 0$ (**Potentiation tail**, pushing $g \to g_{\max} = 1.8$).
  Now compare how your two teaching codes overlap the KC eligibility trace $e(t)$ (generated when the cue is presented at **Frame 2**, i.e., $t \in [0.6, 1.2]$) with $\delta(t)$:
  1. **Adverse Code (DAN pulse IN Frame 2, simultaneous with cue):** During Frame 2 ($t \in [0.6, 1.2]$), KC activity $k(t)$ is high, so $e(t)$ rises rapidly ($\tau_{\text{KC}} = 0.4$) toward $k$. Simultaneously, $r(t)$ is high, so $\delta(t) = L(t) - S(t) < 0$. The product $e(t)\delta(t)$ is **strongly negative** during Frame 2. In Frame 3 ($t \in [1.2, 1.8]$), when $\delta(t)$ flips positive (the rebound tail), the cue is off, so $e(t)$ decays as $e^{-(t-1.2)/0.4}$ (falling by $78\%$ within a single frame!). Thus, the negative integral dominates: **Adverse code produces strong net synaptic depression ($\Delta g < 0$)**.
  2. **Favorable Code (DAN pulse at Frame 0, TWO frames BEFORE the cue at Frame 2):** The DAN pulse occurs at Frame 0 ($t \in [0, 0.6]$). During Frame 0 and Frame 1 ($t \in [0, 1.2]$), the negative phase $\delta(t) < 0$ and the peak of the positive rebound $\delta(t) > 0$ (which for $\tau_S=0.6, \tau_L=1.0$ peaks at $t_{\text{peak}} = \frac{\tau_S \tau_L}{\tau_L - \tau_S}\ln(\tau_L/\tau_S) \approx 0.766$ units after pulse offset, i.e., in **Frame 1** before the cue even arrives!) occur while the KC trace is still zero ($e(t) \approx 0$)! By the time the cue arrives at **Frame 2** ($t = 1.2$, which is $0.6$ units after a 1-frame pulse at Frame 0 ends), $L(t) - S(t)$ is already on its exponential decay tail, AND if $\text{PPL103}$ has any recurrent reverberation during Frame 2, $\delta$ is reduced even further!
* **Crucial Analytical Deduction (Questions 2.4 & 2.5):**
  1. **Sign Convention Check:** In living flies, $\text{MBON12}$ ($\gamma\text{2}\alpha'\text{1}$) is an **approach/attractive** output neuron, and pairing an odor with $\text{PPL103}$ ($\text{PPL1}-\gamma\text{2}\alpha'\text{1}$) aversive shock *depresses* $\text{KC} \rightarrow \text{MBON12}$, reducing attraction [Source](https://elifesciences.org/articles/16135). Because your readout is `score = tanh((MBON12 - MBON01) - unconditioned_balance)` where **higher score is preferred**, a **favorable** outcome requires $g$ to **increase** ($\Delta g > 0$), and an **adverse** outcome requires $g$ to **decrease** ($\Delta g < 0$).
  2. **Asymmetric Potentiation vs. Depression Strength:** Because the Favorable code relies on the decaying tail of $L(t) - S(t)$ two frames ($1.2$ time units) after the DAN pulse onset, whereas the Adverse code overlaps the peak of $e(t)$ directly with the active depression phase $S(t) - L(t)$ in Frame 2, **the magnitude of potentiation $|\Delta g_+|$ per favorable trial is mathematically much smaller than the magnitude of depression $|\Delta g_-|$ per adverse trial** (unless recurrent DAN reverberation alters the waveform)!
  3. **Why Retained Reversal Fails Margin Advantage while Reset Passes:** During Phase 1 (Acquisition), the adverse action's $8$ KCs are strongly depressed toward $g_{\min} = 0.2$, while the favorable action's $8$ KCs are weakly potentiated above $1.0$. When Phase 2 (Reversal) begins in the **Retained** arm, the previously adverse action starts near $g \approx 0.2$ and must climb back above $1.0$ using the weak Favorable tail pulse, while the previously favorable action starts near $1.0$ and is hammered down to $0.2$ by the strong Adverse in-cue pulse! As a result, *both* actions in the Retained Reversal arm end up with depressed gains ($g_A < 1.0, g_B < 1.0$) whose difference $g_A - g_B$ is tiny (yielding the observed margin of $0.00010604$), whereas in the **Reset** arm, $g$ is restored to $1.0$ before Reversal, so the newly adverse action drops sharply from $1.0$ while the newly favorable action stays $\ge 1.0$, creating a large margin!
  4. **Residual $e/S/L$ Interference:** Furthermore, at the end of a $19$-frame episode ($t = 11.4$), residual $L(11.4) \propto e^{-10.2/1.0} \approx 3.7 \times 10^{-5}$ is tiny *unless* recurrent network activity keeps $\text{PPL103}$ firing above baseline in the tail frames! Because you noted that **neural state $h_j$ is reset at the episode boundary, whereas persistent plastic states ($g, e, S, L$) are NOT reset across lessons** (they were only reset in the artificial whole-memory reset branch), any tail activity in Frame 18 leaves non-zero $e, S, L$ at Frame 0 of the *next* lesson! Specifically, a leftover $e > 0$ from Lesson $n$ will be multiplied by the Frame 0 Favorable DAN pulse ($\delta < 0$) at the very start of Lesson $n+1$, **inappropriately depressing the previous lesson's KCs whenever the next lesson is Favorable!**

**3.5. Causal Role of Disabled, Absent, and Shuffled Controls (Question 2.7):**
* Because `score = tanh(balance - unconditioned_balance)` subtracts the exact unconditioned trajectory for the identical cue, **Disabled Plasticity ($g_i \equiv 1.0$) produces `score == 0.0` for all actions by algebraic identity**, resulting in $100\%$ ties. * **Independent Unit of Analysis:** The **Position Family $f \in \{1, \dots, F\}$**, where each family $f$ is generated from an independent base tactical configuration and contains a matched minimal pair $(P_f^+, P_f^-)$ sharing the same focal candidate move $a_{\text{focal}}$ and safe reference move $a_{\text{safe}}$ (plus distractor/broken-pin variants $P_f^{\text{ctrl}}$).
* **Continuous Primary Outcome (Solves the G0 Margin Sensitivity Problem):** For each family $f$ and projection seed $s$, define the **Paired Counterfactual Contrast Margin**:
  $$\Delta M_{f,s} = \left[\text{score}_s(P_f^+, a_{\text{focal}}) - \text{score}_s(P_f^+, a_{\text{safe}})\right] - \left[\text{score}_s(P_f^-, a_{\text{focal}}) - \text{score}_s(P_f^-, a_{\text{safe}})\right]$$
  Why is $\Delta M_{f,s}$ vastly superior to raw accuracy? Because **any fixed bias** toward $a_{\text{focal}}$ (or any global shift in `MBON12`) cancels out identically in the subtraction $\text{Diff}(P_f^+) - \text{Diff}(P_f^-)$! Under Disabled Plasticity or any state-blind heuristic, $\Delta M_{f,s} \equiv 0$ by algebraic identity.
* **Binary Secondary Outcome (Strict Family Pass $Y_{f,s} \in \{0, 1\}$):**
  $$Y_{f,s} = \mathbb{I}\Big(\text{score}_s(P_f^+, a_{\text{focal}}) > \text{score}_s(P_f^+, a_{\text{safe}}) \;\wedge\; \text{score}_s(P_f^-, a_{\text{focal}}) < \text{score}_s(P_f^-, a_{\text{safe}})\Big)$$
* **Treatment of Ties and Unknowns:**
  * *Ties ($\text{score}_1 == \text{score}_2$ within float32 tolerance $10^{-7}$):* Count as **0 (Failure)** for the binary indicator $Y_{f,s}$, and contribute $0.0$ to $\Delta M_{f,s}$. Never break ties randomly in favor of the learner.
  * *Unknown Verification:* By construction, deterministic 2-to-3-ply minimax on $\le 6$-piece boards has **zero** unknown outcomes during dataset generation. Any candidate board whose verification exceeds resource bounds or lacks a unique consequence separation is rejected *during corpus generation* before training begins. If an online trajectory encounters an unverified state, zero teaching episode is emitted and the event is logged as an audit exception.

**12.2. Null Distribution of Family Pass Rates and All-Legal Ranking (Questions 8.2 & 8.3):**
* **When is the null probability of a 4-member family quadruple equal to $(1/2)^4 = 1/16$? (Question 8.2):** **Only** if the model makes $4$ statistically independent, unbiased $50/50$ coin flips across the $4$ boards! In a real neural network, decisions within a family are **strongly correlated**: if a model has a fixed bias toward capturing ($P(\text{choose } a_{\text{focal}}) = p$), then on a pair $(P_f^+, P_f^-)$ where $P_f^+$ requires choosing $a_{\text{focal}}$ and $P_f^-$ requires rejecting $a_{\text{focal}}$, the probability of passing *both* under an independent bias $p$ is $p(1-p) \le 0.25$! And if $P_f^+$ and $P_f^-$ share $90\%$ of their KCs so the network outputs the *same* preference sign on both boards with correlation $\rho > 0$, the null pass rate on the opposite-direction pair $(P_f^+, P_f^-)$ is **strictly less than $0.25$** (approaching $0$ as $\rho \to 1$)! Conversely, adding two positive controls ($P_{\text{pos}}, P_{\text{distractor-pos}}$) that require the *same* action as $P_{\text{pos}}$ adds almost no penalty when $\rho \to 1$. That is why **comparing against the empirical Shuffled Feedback control on the exact same families** is mandatory instead of relying on a theoretical $(1/2)^4$ binomial null!
* **Does all-legal ranking with $k \ge 10$ moves have chance $< 10^{-4}$? (Question 8.3):** **No!** If a position has $k = 15$ legal moves (like Position 1A), and $1$ move wins material (`a1a5`, $+3$), $12$ moves are neutral quiet moves ($\Delta M = 0$), and $2$ moves lose the Rook ($\Delta M = -5$):
  * In $P_f^+$ (Undefended), `a1a5` is the **uniquely best move** ($1/15$ chance under uniform random ranking).
  * In $P_f^-$ (Defended), `a1a5` loses material ($\Delta M = -2$), so **any of the $12$ safe neutral moves** is an acceptable optimal choice ($12/15 = 80\%$ chance under uniform random ranking)!
  * Therefore, under uniform random ranking across $(P_f^+, P_f^-)$, the probability of picking the unique winner in $P_f^+$ AND any safe move in $P_f^-$ is $\frac{1}{15} \times \frac{12}{15} = \frac{12}{225} \approx 0.0533$ ($5.3\%$), **not** $< 10^{-4}$! Always compute the exact combinatoric null $p_{\text{null}}(f) = \frac{|\mathcal{A}_{\text{opt}}(P_f^+)|}{|\mathcal{L}(P_f^+)|} \times \frac{|\mathcal{A}_{\text{opt}}(P_f^-)|}{|\mathcal{L}(P_f^-)|}$ for each family $f$.

**12.3. Multiplicity Control, Checkpoint Selection, and Sample Size Derivation (Questions 8.4 & 8.5):**
* **Checkpoint Selection without Test Leakage:** Select the winning training checkpoint $c^* \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ **exclusively on the Validation Split** by maximizing mean validation contrast margin $\overline{\Delta M}_{\text{val}}$. Freeze $c^*$ and evaluate the **Sealed Final Split** *exactly once* at $c^*$. Because only $1$ checkpoint is evaluated on the Final Split, checkpoint selection requires zero Bonferroni penalty on the Final Split test!
* **Multiplicity Across Controls (Intersection-Union Gate):** To advance a stage, the Informative arm must beat Shuffled Feedback AND beat Absent Feedback AND beat Disabled Plasticity simultaneously. Under the **Intersection-Union Test (IUT)** principle in mathematical statistics, when a composite pass requires rejecting every individual null $H_{0,m}: \mu_{\text{Info}} \le \mu_{\text{Control } m}$ for all $m \in \{1,2,3\}$ in the *same* predetermined direction, testing each comparison at level $\alpha$ guarantees that the overall family-wise Type I error rate of falsely passing the gate is **at most $\alpha$** (in fact, $\le \alpha$ without Bonferroni division!).
* **Deriving Sample Size $F$ (Families) and $S$ (Seeds) from Pilot Variance (Question 8.5):**
  Let $D_{f,s} = \Delta M_{f,s}^{(\text{Info})} - \Delta M_{f,s}^{(\text{Shuffled})}$ be the paired difference on family $f$ and seed $s$. Using a variance-components model $D_{f,s} = \mu_D + u_f + v_s + \epsilon_{f,s}$, the variance of the grand mean $\bar{D}$ across $F$ independent families and $S$ projection seeds is:
  $$\text{Var}(\bar{D}) = \frac{\sigma_{\text{family}}^2}{F} + \frac{\sigma_{\text{seed}}^2}{S} + \frac{\sigma_{\text{error}}^2}{F \cdot S}$$
  To achieve power $1 - \beta$ at one-sided significance level $\alpha$, we need:
  $$\frac{\mu_D}{\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{\text{family}}^2}{F} + \frac{\sigma_{\text{seed}}^2}{S} + \frac{\sigma_{\text{error}}^2}{F \cdot S}}} \ge z_{1-\alpha} + z_{1-\beta}$$
  * *Illustrative Hypothetical Calculation (to be replaced by your Validation split variance estimate):* Suppose on the $16$-board Validation split across $S = 3$ seeds, you observe standardized effect components $\sigma_{\text{family}} / \mu_D \approx 1.0$, $\sigma_{\text{seed}} / \mu_D \approx 0.4$, and $\sigma_{\text{error}} / \mu_D \approx 1.2$. Then with $S = 3$ seeds and $F = 16$ final families ($64$ boards total, matching your existing $64$-board final quota!), $\text{SE}(\bar{D})/\mu_D = \sqrt{1/16 + 0.16/3 + 1.44/48} = \sqrt{0.0625 + 0.0533 + 0.0300} = 0.382$, giving $t \approx 1 / 0.382 = 2.62$ ($p \approx 0.008$, power $\approx 80\%$ at $\alpha = 0.025$). Notice how your existing quota of **64 final boards ($F = 16$ minimal-pair quadruples) across $S = 3$ projection seeds** is statistically well-powered *provided* seed-to-seed variance $\sigma_{\text{seed}}$ is controlled via the paired contrast $\Delta M_{f,s}$!

**12.4. Preventing Adaptive Overfitting to Sealed Final Families Across Failed Studies (Question 8.7):**
If a "sealed" Final Test set is evaluated at the end of Study 1, Study 1 fails, the researcher changes the encoder for Study 2, and evaluates the *same* Final Test set again, the Final Test set leaks information through the researcher's adaptive loop (Dwork et al., 2015, reusable holdout problem).
* **Two-Tier Holdout & Algorithmic Family Generator Solution:**
  1. **Never touch the Final Test split when a Validation Gate fails!** In your automation script, add a hard guard: `if not validation_gate_passed: skip_final_test_evaluation()`. If a mechanism or encoder fails on the Validation split (or on G0), the Final Test split remains $100\%$ unrun and uninspected.
  2. **Parametric Relational Family Generator with Seeded Holdout Partitions:** Because $\le 6$-piece tactical relations (Stage 1–3) are defined by exact geometric constraints (Attacker ray + Target square + Defender vector + Pin ray + Distractor square), write a deterministic generator `generate_families(stage, partition_seed)` that enumerates all valid coordinate/piece instantiations and partitions them by hashing the canonical `(target_file_group, piece_combo)` into **Development Validation**, **Confirmatory Final Set A**, and **Reserve Confirmatory Final Set B**. If a run passes Validation but fails Final Set A, any redesigned mechanism must be confirmed on **Final Set B** with a fresh registration.
