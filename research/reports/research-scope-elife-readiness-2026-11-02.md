## eLife scope recommendation

Official scope includes computational methods/models that provide biological insights. Editors select submissions for review; selected papers become Reviewed Preprints, with public assessment rather than an accept/reject decision after review. Sources accessed 2026-11-02: https://elifesciences.org/about/aims-scope and https://elifesciences.org/about/submit-your-research . This does not guarantee editorial selection.

Recommended first-paper question, generated here as a proposal: **Which representational and plasticity constraints determine whether a fly-connectome-derived model retains and revises conflicting associations, and which circuit interventions predictably change that tradeoff?**

This is presently closer to the causal evidence than a general reasoning paper. A strong computational-only contribution could connect a defined circuit mechanism to behavioral predictions and reproduce relevant published conditioning results. New animal experiments are not asserted to be a universal eLife requirement; an experimentally testable prediction is a valuable bridge. Pure exact-cue external lookup improves the system but does not itself explain fly biology.

Keep Volume 3 as a separate, permitted computational track. If hybrid transfer succeeds, test where the advantage originates. A useful engineered result without a biological insight may need a different publication scope; that is a scientific outcome, not a failed reason to continue indefinitely.

## What could invalidate the story?

- Lookup or a simple raw-input learner matches transfer: no incremental connectome claim.
- Performance survives relevant memory removal: the claimed learned mechanism is unsupported.
- Neural and hybrid conditions differ in supervision, exposure or compute: attribution is confounded.
- A repeated familiar cue succeeds but unseen structures fail: retrieval evidence only.
- Biological conclusions depend on the external store, KC rank relay, arbitrary score signs or CPU rest: scope exceeds evidence.
- One intervention case is generalized to all seeds or biology: causal scope is overstated.

Recommendation: pursue one coherent causal claim for the first submission, retain all five volumes as the broader program, and treat Phase C reasoning as an empirical goal rather than the promised paper conclusion. No direction or gate is changed by this recommendation.
