## Mushroom body anatomy

Yoshinori Aso and colleagues, 2014.
[The neuronal architecture of the mushroom body provides a logic for associative learning](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04577).
DOI (a permanent paper identifier): 10.7554/eLife.04577. The mushroom body is part of the fly's learning circuit.
Associative learning means learning a relation between experiences.
Authors: Aso, Hattori, Yu, Johnston, Iyer, Ngo, Dionne, Abbott, Axel, Tanimoto, and Rubin.

The reviewer read the paper summary, the MBON05 row in Table 1, and the explanations and text for Figures 9 and 17. A figure explanation describes what a figure shows. The saved notes record where these passages appeared on the web page.
The paper supports the name MBON05, the chemical it uses to send signals, and which brain areas it connects to.
A transmitter is a chemical a neuron uses to send signals. A projection is a connection from one brain area to another.
The paper does not establish every target's response, this project's exact cell IDs, or a chess-score sign.
It does not show that the four-value memory rule in our code matches a living fly or that computer time matches time in a fly.
Figure images and raw recordings were not independently checked. The publisher page opened.
Searches within it used MBON-gamma4 and glutamate.
