L10: # eLife Assessments
L11: 
L12: Articles in eLife provide readers with a more nuanced assessment of the research being reported.
L13: 
L14: eLife changed its approach to peer review in 2023 to better convey the assessments made by editors and reviewers. Every Reviewed Preprint published in the journal now includes an eLife Assessment written by the editor who oversaw the review process and the peer reviewers. This assessment summarizes what the editor and reviewers thought about the article.
L15: eLife Assessments also use a common vocabulary to describe the significance of the findings reported in the paper (on a scale ranging from useful to landmark) and the strength of evidence (on a scale ranging from inadequate to exceptional).
L16: 
L17: The definitions of the terms used for the significance of the findings and the strength of evidence are given below.
L18: ##### Significance of the findings:
L19: 
L20:   * Landmark: findings with profound implications that are expected to have widespread influence
L21: 
L22:   * Fundamental: findings that substantially advance our understanding of major research questions
L23: 
L24:   * Important: findings that have theoretical or practical implications beyond a single subfield
L25: 
L26:   * Valuable: findings that have theoretical or practical implications for a subfield
L27: 
L28:   * Useful: findings that have focused importance and scope
L29: ##### Strength of evidence:
L30: 
L31:   * Exceptional: exemplary use of existing approaches that establish new standards for a field
L32: 
L33:   * Compelling: evidence that features methods, data and analyses more rigorous than the current state-of-the-art
L34: 
L35:   * Convincing: appropriate and validated methodology in line with current state-of-the-art
L36: 
L37:   * Solid: methods, data and analyses broadly support the claims with only minor weaknesses
L38: 
L39:   * Incomplete: main claims are only partially supported
L40:   * Inadequate: methods, data and analyses do not support the primary claims

L5: # For Authors: Updates from eLife’s Ethics Committee
L6: 
L7: An update on eLife’s Ethics Committee’s recent efforts and future plans.
L8: 
L9:   * 335 views
L10: 
L11: Dec 15, 2022
L12: 
L13:   *   * Open annotations (there are currently 0 annotations on this page).
L14: 
L15: By the eLife Ethics Committee
L16: eLife is committed to supporting researchers to publish their work openly and transparently while following best research and ethical practice. The role of the eLife Ethics Committee is to establish and develop journal policies relating to publishing ethics and advise on a vast array of ethical issues as they arise. Here we provide an update about the Ethics Committee’s recent efforts and future plans.
L17: ### Data availability
L18: We have updated our 【2†data sharing policies】 to provide more detailed guidance for authors and to clearly set out requirements for the public release of data in research that eLife reviews and publishes. This includes mechanisms of data sharing, timing of data release, compliance with local laws and requirements, and handling of situations where data sharing may be restricted. From next year, eLife will be 【3†eliminating accept/reject decisions】 after peer review and publishing Reviewed Preprints.
L19: There are plans to add badges to the Reviewed Preprint so it is easy for readers to know what criteria the work has, or has not yet, satisfied in terms of open data, open materials and preregistration. When publishing a Version of Record, we will still check that the data, methods and code are made available.
L20: ### Image integrity
L21: 
L22: The eLife Ethics Committee has recently created detailed internal documentation for editors and staff to help them handle cases where concerns arise about the integrity of images in a published eLife article. With this guidance they can decide whether the publication of a notice, such as a Correction, Expression of Concern or Retraction, is warranted.
L23: eLife has also recently introduced the following submission requirements to assist authors with including the correct information in figures, and to improve image preparation and data visualisation:
L24:   * Include scale bars for micrographs, and molecular weight markers for gels and blots.
L25:   * Provide information about data processing and analysis in their figure legends and/or the main article. For example, statistical tests applied, sample number, p values of tests, replicates, and so on.
L26:   * Provide the original raw gel and blot images for the underlying figures in the manuscript as Source data.
L27: ### Ethical provenance of materials
L28: 
L29: eLife now follows the guidance set forth in 【4†a letter from the Society of Vertebrate Paleontology†vertpaleo.org】 (SVP) when considering research involving Myanmar amber fossils. In support of the SVP guidance, which is based on recommendations from the scientific community, eLife has introduced new submission checks to confirm the ethical provenance of Myanmar amber specimens and will not consider submissions that don’t meet these requirements.
L30: ### Parachute science
L31: Also known as helicopter science, this describes the practice whereby researchers from wealthy institutions carry out fieldwork or institutional visits in lower-income countries or otherwise use data or resources from these countries without meaningfully collaborating with local researchers or involving the local communities. This often becomes apparent when the work is published with no, or minor, authorship roles attributed to local researchers.
L32: There have been ongoing internal and external discussions involving the Ethics Committee and eLife’s Communities team to identify active steps that eLife can take to address parachute science. We welcome comments from those affected by this issue.
L33: ### Future plans
L34: We will be exploring the issue of parachute science, including discussing a potential magazine piece to address the issue. We will also finalise an anti-parachute science policy for eLife that is based on the policy on 【5†inclusivity in global research†theplosblog.plos.org】 implemented across all PLOS journals.
L35: Our hope is to raise awareness and encourage all authors to carefully think about the ethical considerations, permissions and authorship decisions that they have made in relation to equity and inclusion in global research.
L36: Other potential topics the committee will explore include: (1) a “pandemic pathogens policy” for research where there may be implications for biosafety, biosecurity; (2) potential dual-use research of concern; (3) “unrepresented, vulnerable populations” and their role/involvement in research; (4) falsified research case studies, and journal and institutional approaches to handling misconduct; (5) journal carbon footprint – including data policies for very large datasets.
L37: The Ethics Committee will next meet in early 2023 when we will continue our remit to discuss relevant ethical issues, and identify where and how the committee can raise awareness or take the lead in policy development. We will also be exploring ways to increase the coverage of publishing ethics and related issues in eLife (by, for example, encouraging the submission of magazine content and blog-post writing).
L38: We welcome suggestions from the community for other issues that the ethics committee should consider.
