# Adaptive near-transfer probe

After the one-puzzle adaptive contrastive condition passed familiar acquired repair, I tested whether its saved memory ranked the certified move better on five unseen queen/rook variations. The positions share the original smothered-mate motif and remain development-only. I compared the trained memory to the same run's absent, shuffled, and disabled-plasticity memories, with all legal actions and no feedback during evaluation.

The trained memory moved the certified success above every control ranking on all five positions, including rank 2/34 versus control ranks 28-32 on queen d5/rook f8. Yet it chose a verified failure in all five. This is consistent relative-rank transfer within one motif, but not correct-choice transfer, an unfamiliar-family result, or reasoning. Each panel preserved its memory; independent audit checked 636 all-legal certificates and candidate rows.

The first three source versions had setup/parser failures before candidate panels; all are preserved. The v4 runner completed. Its first auditor failed on a nested certificate-hash path; the failure source is preserved, and a separate hash-pinned correction protocol produced the passing audit. No raw run report or frozen protocol was rewritten. Full evidence: [v4 report](../../../archive/early-development/research/reports/adaptive-near-transfer-v4-2026-10-07/README.md).
