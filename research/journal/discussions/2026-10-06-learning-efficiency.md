# 2026-10-06: First reasoning breakthrough and subsequent learning efficiency

The researcher asked to document a hypothesis and calculation for a future research diagram: the first verified reasoning ability may take hours to acquire, analogous to teaching a child to solve a Rubik's Cube for the first time; subsequent success could become faster and more reliable through training.

Record this as researcher intent and an untested expectation. Repeated success alone demonstrates reliability, not causal training or transferable reasoning. Separate fresh-seed replication, continued practice/retention from persistent memory, and reduced experience needed for a new related ability. The stronger hypothesis is that reusable learned relationships make later learning easier; exponential acceleration is not assumed.

The [learning-efficiency design note](../../../documentation/design/research-goals/learning-efficiency.md) proposes fresh-versus-prior-memory experience/time ratios and a two-panel research figure, with matched feedback controls, disjoint families, frozen evaluation, paired seeds, uncertainty and explicit budget-exhaustion handling. Its numerical example is invented arithmetic only. Hours to first breakthrough, transfer and acceleration remain unmeasured. These are proposed operational definitions, not a frozen or executed protocol.

Documentation only: no neural code, ongoing experiment, acceptance gate or raw report changed. Existing puzzle-learning failures remain preserved; useful acquisition and unfamiliar/counterfactual reasoning are still unproved.
