## Subject

Student mentorship inquiry: reward delivery in an adult fly model

## Email

Dear Professor Claridge-Chang,

I'm Ethan Ramli, a 16-year-old Singaporean grade 11 student in Jakarta. I enjoy chess, and discovering the public adult fly connectome made me wonder whether a model using that wiring could learn to make better decisions through experience and feedback.

Your group's work on synthetic memory induction and dopamine's effects on attraction and aversion connects to something I'm trying to figure out: how should I give the model feedback so it learns something useful for unfamiliar positions, rather than just repeating answers it has seen? I also want to understand how to tell a lasting memory apart from an immediate response to stimulation.

In all six simple tests, the model preferred the rewarded option more after training. But when I changed which option earned the reward, it chose the other option in only three tests. So it failed the full test. I haven't established whether the mechanism is biologically sound, and the chess part currently records feedback without learning from it.

I define the research question and training rubric, AI makes most of the code, and I am still learning the underlying methods. I have no research adviser. I'd like to develop this into a sound study that I could eventually submit to eLife.

Would you be open to occasional mentorship, starting with how to deliver feedback and test whether it leads to lasting learning? I'd appreciate your honest view of the approach. I can send a one-page methods summary first.

Thank you for your time,
Ethan Ramli
High-school student | Jakarta, Indonesia
