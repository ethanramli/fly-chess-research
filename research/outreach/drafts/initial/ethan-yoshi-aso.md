## Subject

Student mentorship inquiry: memory updating in an adult fly model

## Email

Dear Dr Aso,

I'm Ethan Ramli, a 16-year-old Singaporean grade 11 student in Jakarta. Chess got me interested in decision-making, and the public adult fly connectome gave me an idea: could a model using that wiring learn to make useful chess decisions from puzzles and feedback?

Your work with Gerald Rubin on how different dopamine neurons write and update memories seems especially useful for a problem I'm stuck on. I need to understand which reinforcement pathways to use and when to give feedback, particularly when a previously rewarded choice is no longer rewarded.

In the current gamma4 association prototype, preference for the rewarded cue strengthened in all six test runs. But when the reward switched to the other cue, only three runs made the correct new choice, so the full test failed. I'm unsure whether the problem is the learning rule, feedback timing, or training experience. The mechanism isn't biologically validated, and the chess part still only records feedback.

I define the research question and training rubric, AI makes most of the code, and I am still learning the underlying methods. I don't have a research adviser and would like to turn this into a sound study, with the hope of eventually submitting it to eLife.

Would you consider occasional mentorship, starting with the learning mechanism and this reversal problem? I'd value an honest assessment of whether the approach makes sense. I can send a one-page methods summary first.

Thank you for considering it,
Ethan Ramli
High-school student | Jakarta, Indonesia
