## Purpose

This folder supports careful, individualized requests for limited scientific guidance on the FlyChess project. It is not a bulk-email list. Every contact route, role, and research claim must be checked against a current official source immediately before use.

## Evidence boundary to preserve

- The project is a from-scratch, connectome-constrained simulation using chess as a controlled task environment.
- The current gamma4 work is an engineered association prototype, not a reconstructed natural reward pathway.
- The balanced six-seed follow-up strengthened the rewarded preference in 6/6 runs, but only 3/6 made the correct reversed choice. The complete learning gate therefore failed.
- A record-only puzzle round selected a mate-in-one and recorded feedback, but did not apply feedback to neurons or learn.
- Biological sensory, action, reward, and plasticity interfaces and held-out chess transfer remain unresolved.
- Do not state or imply that a fly understands chess, that biological learning has been reproduced, or that any contacted researcher endorses the work.

## Dossier acceptance checklist

Each expert file should include:

1. Current role, institution, timezone, and an official profile.
2. A currently verified public professional contact route, or `not found`.
3. At least two concrete papers, datasets, or active projects relevant to the outreach.
4. A precise explanation of project fit and at least one reason the person may not be the best contact.
5. One bounded question that can be answered without accepting supervision responsibility.
6. Relevant junior or assistant researchers, when supported by current official evidence.
7. A personalized draft of roughly 130-190 words with an honest student-status placeholder.
8. Direct source links and an access date.

## Sending gate

Before any draft is sent:

- re-open the official profile and verify the role and address;
- replace all placeholders and confirm the student's preferred name, school/status, location, and project link;
- verify the referenced paper or project was actually read closely enough to mention accurately;
- check that the request is small and specific;
- review the final recipient, subject, body, and any attachment with the user;
- log the contact date and follow-up date;
- send individually, never as a bulk or Bcc campaign.
