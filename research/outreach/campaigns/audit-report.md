## Email-body audit

- **Length:** All 56 drafts fall within 130–190 body words under the count method above. The guide recommends a 120–180-word practical target (`cold-email-guide.md:29-35`), while the dossier checklist requests 130–190 (`README.md:23-29`); these are overlapping but not identical standards. Pick one editorial target for future drafts.
- **Template feel:** The drafts are mostly individualized in recipient-specific work references and questions, but several reuse near-identical project-status paragraphs and student-status placeholders. That repetition can make a batch feel templated if sent unchanged. Keep each opening tied to the recipient’s specific paper or method and retain only the project facts needed for that one question. This is a moderate style risk, not evidence that messages were sent in bulk.
- **Irrelevant controversy/retraction:** The retraction reference identified in Anna Cook’s email body was removed. Scott Waddell’s dossier accurately labels the 2023 paper’s retraction in the work inventory; the status should stay attached if that paper is discussed, but it need not be raised in a first-contact message unless directly relevant to the ask.
- **Asks:** Most asks are one-method, one-control, or one-measurement questions. The six proposed below are similarly bounded. Keep the asks separate from any request for mentorship, placement, or a referral, consistent with `cold-email-guide.md:39-45` and `outreach-index.md:55-67`.
- **Praise and salutation:** I found no clear case of unsupported prestige-based praise in the email bodies. Salutations are generally consistent with the stated titles or use a first name where no title is asserted. Recheck titles and preferred names against current official pages before sending; Ceffa’s current Visiting Researcher role is the specific title update noted above.
- **Overclaiming:** Drafts and the one-page summary generally describe the work as engineered/engineering evidence and explicitly state the failed reversal gate and record-only puzzle feedback. Keep those qualifications: do not describe the association prototype as validated biological learning or the puzzle round as learning. The summary states this accurately at `project-one-page-summary.md:22-29`.
