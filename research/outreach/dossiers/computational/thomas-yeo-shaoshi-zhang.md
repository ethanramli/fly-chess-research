# Shaoshi Zhang

## Snapshot

The latest official NUS Neuroimaging roster located lists Zhang as a Research Fellow; that page is dated 2024, so confidence in the role being current on 2026-09-30 is medium. The team page does not publish a direct personal email; the project contact page lists Yeo's public NUS address, `thomas.yeo@nus.edu.sg`, as a route into the group. Singapore is SGT (UTC+8). ([NUS roster](https://medicine.nus.edu.sg/maccc/neuroimaging/), [NUS contact page](https://medicine.nus.edu.sg/trp/human-potential/projects/machine-learning-of-brain-mri-to-improve-mental-health-and-disorder/), [timezone](https://www.timeanddate.com/worldclock/singapore/singapore))

## Why relevant

Zhang is a lead author of work using human imaging to estimate a whole-cortex excitation/inhibition marker and relate it to maturation and cognition. That provides a methodological point of contact for asking how to validate a model-derived neural quantity; it does not transfer directly to fly synapses. ([PNAS paper](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2318641121))

## Current/recent work

- Zhang led the 2024 PNAS paper “In vivo whole-cortex marker of excitation-inhibition ratio indexes cortical maturation and cognitive ability in youth,” coauthored by Thomas Yeo. ([PNAS, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2318641121))
- In 2025, Zhang was a joint first author of the Nature paper “Longer scans boost prediction and cut costs in brain-wide association studies,” with Yeo as senior/corresponding author. ([Nature, 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09250-1), [NUS release](https://medicine.nus.edu.sg/news/global-study-shows-longer-brain-scans-lower-research-costs-provide-more-accurate-predictions/))

## Specific overlap with this project

FlyChess also has a model-derived circuit response, but its engineered gamma4 readout and learning mechanism have not cleared acceptance: correct reversal choices occurred in only 3/6 seeds. A question about validation and measurement limits is appropriate; the project does not claim its neural response contrast is a biological behavioral variable. ([project validation](../../../../documentation/design/neural-learning/gamma4-association-tests.md), [mechanism limitations](../../../../documentation/design/neural-learning/gamma4-plasticity.md))

## What to ask

What independent measurement or held-out test would you prioritize before interpreting a model-derived neural response marker as evidence of a meaningful biological computation?

## Reasons they may not be the best contact

The cited work concerns human MRI and cortical maturation, not insect circuits, synaptic plasticity or reward learning. A fly learning researcher may be better placed to assess biological mechanism. ([PNAS paper](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2318641121))

## Personalized email draft

**Subject:** A question about validating a model-derived circuit response

Dear Dr. Zhang,

I’m a [grade/year] student working independently on a connectome-constrained fly simulation. I read your 2024 PNAS study on an in vivo whole-cortex excitation/inhibition marker and was interested in the problem of validating a model-derived neural measure against an independent behavioral or biological test.

Our project has an engineered gamma4 association prototype with persistent local efficacy, but the natural reward and sensory/action pathways are not established. Its full acceptance test failed: only 3 of 6 seeds selected the newly rewarded cue after reversal. Separately, our puzzle runner records feedback without applying learning, and we have not tested chess transfer. We are treating the MBON response contrast as a software measurement, not as behavior or proof of biological learning.

Could you recommend one independent measurement or held-out test that would be most useful before interpreting such a response marker as a meaningful computation? A brief pointer to a paper or design principle would already help. I can share a concise protocol if useful.
