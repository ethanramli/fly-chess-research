# Ann Hermundstad

## Snapshot

As of 2026-09-30, Ann Hermundstad is a Group Leader at HHMI's Janelia Research Campus and Head of Theory@HHMI. Her official personal site lists `hermundstada@janelia.hhmi.org`; a Janelia paper also lists her as corresponding author. Ashburn, Virginia was on Eastern Daylight Time (EDT, UTC−4) on the access date. Confidence: high for current role and email; high for date-specific timezone. ([HHMI role announcement](https://www.hhmi.org/research/janelia/ann-hermundstad), [Janelia profile](https://www.janelia.org/people/ann-hermundstad), [official contact page](https://www.annmh.com/contact.html), [Ashburn timezone](https://www.timeanddate.com/time/zone/@4744870))

## Why relevant

Her lab studies how animals build and adapt internal sensorimotor representations under uncertainty, combining theory and data with experimental collaborators. One recent fly study examined rapid reinforcement-driven goal learning and the role of head-direction circuits, directly relevant to the project's still-unresolved separation of sensory input, choice, and feedback. ([Hermundstad Lab](https://www.janelia.org/lab/hermundstad-lab), [2024 Neuron paper](https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273(24)00326-X))

## Current/recent work

- In May 2024, Hermundstad coauthored “A neural circuit architecture for rapid learning in goal-directed navigation,” which studied Drosophila goal-heading learning under selective thermal reinforcement and modeled interactions between head-direction and goal representations. ([Neuron, 2024](https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273(24)00326-X))
- In September 2025, her group posted “Composing trajectories for rapid inference of navigational goals,” a mouse navigation modeling study using rewarded and unrewarded trajectories to infer useful spatial anchors and adapt to target switches. It was a preprint as of access. ([Janelia publication record](https://www.janelia.org/publication/composing-trajectories-for-rapid-inference-of-navigational-goals))
- In 2026, HHMI named her Head of the new Theory@HHMI initiative. ([HHMI announcement](https://www.hhmi.org/news/meet-ann-hermundstad-leader-janelias-newest-research-area-theory))

## Specific overlap with this project

FlyChess uses a fixed connectome and an engineered gamma4 association mechanism; natural sensory, action and reward interfaces are unresolved. The full acceptance gate failed because only 3/6 seeds chose correctly after reversal. Her 2024 fly study is relevant to how reinforcement and internal representations are linked in an actual insect task, and her 2025 work offers ideas for representing prior rewarded and unrewarded trajectories. Neither establishes that a fly understands chess or validates the project. ([project learning direction](../../../../documentation/design/research-goals/feedback-learning.md), [association validation](../../../../documentation/design/neural-learning/gamma4-association-tests.md), [2024 Neuron paper](https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273(24)00326-X))

## What to ask

For a connectome-constrained model whose feedback path is not yet biologically mapped, what is one minimal behavioral or neural test you would require before calling a persistent response change feedback-dependent learning?

## Reasons she may not be the best contact

Her strongest overlap is theory and goal-directed navigation, not chess or mushroom-body plasticity specifically. Her 2024 fly paper studies central-complex/head-direction circuitry and heat reinforcement, while FlyChess currently has an engineered mushroom-body association prototype. A mushroom-body experimentalist may be better for validating those anatomical assumptions. ([2024 Neuron paper](https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273(24)00326-X), [project mechanism note](../../../../documentation/design/neural-learning/gamma4-plasticity.md))

## Personalized email draft

**Subject:** One validation test for feedback-dependent learning in a fly model

Dear Dr. Hermundstad,

I’m a [grade/year] student working independently on a connectome-constrained fly simulation. Your 2024 Neuron study of rapid goal learning in Drosophila stood out to me because it connects reinforcement, internal representations and circuit function in a real behavioral task.

My project is much earlier and narrower. It has an engineered gamma4 association prototype, but biological reward, sensory and action mappings remain unresolved. In a frozen choice experiment, only 3 of 6 seeds chose correctly after contingency reversal, so the full acceptance gate failed. Our puzzle runner currently records feedback without applying neural learning, and chess transfer has not been tested. I am not claiming that flies understand chess or that this mechanism is biologically validated.

Could you suggest one minimal behavioral or neural test that would be necessary before describing a persistent response change as feedback-dependent learning? I can send a one-page summary of the protocol and failed reversal result if helpful.

Thank you for your time.

Best,
