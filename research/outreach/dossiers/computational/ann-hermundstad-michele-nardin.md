## Why relevant

Nardin's official profile describes work on how learning changes neural representations and how physiological needs shape behavior and information flow. Those themes are useful for asking whether a state change persists and what behavior-level evidence should accompany it. ([Janelia profile](https://www.janelia.org/people/michele-nardin))

## Current/recent work

- In March 2025, Nardin coauthored “Learning reshapes the hippocampal representation hierarchy,” a PNAS study of how learning changes representational structure in hippocampal populations. ([PNAS, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2417025122))
- In April 2024, Nardin coauthored a Neuron study on hippocampal CCK-expressing interneurons, temporal coding and contextual learning. ([Neuron, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2024.03.019))
- His current Janelia role is Theory Fellow in the Hermundstad Lab. ([Current lab roster](https://www.janelia.org/lab/hermundstad-lab/members))

## Specific overlap with this project

The project's gamma4 mechanism stores engineered local efficacy; the full validation gate failed at 3/6 correct reversal choices, and biological reward/action mappings and chess transfer remain unresolved. Nardin's work is relevant to relating changes in neural representations to learning and behavior, while the project must avoid treating persistence alone as biological retention or a neural-response contrast as choice.
