## Why relevant

Stürner studies how innate and learned pathways converge and has contributed to connectomic work spanning ascending sensory input and descending output. That makes her work relevant to the project's missing interface between task input, neural state, and selected action.

## Current/recent work

- **2025 — “Comparative connectomics of Drosophila descending and ascending neurons,” Nature.** Integrates adult brain and nerve-cord datasets to map sensorimotor pathways and compare connectivity across sexes. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08925-z).
- **2024 — “Systematic annotation of a complete adult male Drosophila nerve cord connectome reveals principles of functional organisation,” eLife.** A companion resource for interpreting the male ventral nerve cord and its sensory, ascending, and motor pathways. [Article](https://elifesciences.org/articles/97766).
- **2024 — “Whole-brain annotation and multi-connectome cell typing of Drosophila,” Nature.** A collaborative cell-type annotation resource that includes Stürner. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07686-5).

## Specific overlap with this project

The project models the adult brain without justified sensory-to-board or neural-to-action mappings. Stürner's work illustrates how connectomic paths can be traced across brain and nerve cord and compared against functional labels. It does not validate the project's feedback-learning rule or the idea that chess is a biologically meaningful task.

## What to ask

What is one defensible way to test whether a simulated action readout is supported by the connectome rather than imposed by the model designer?

## Reasons they may not be the best contact
