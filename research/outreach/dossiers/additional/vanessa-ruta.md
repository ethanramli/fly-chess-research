**FlyChess fit:** The 2019 study provides an unusually close empirical comparison for the project’s unresolved reversal problem; the 2024 work adds a circuit-level perspective on flexible cue-to-action routing. Neither establishes a biological chess interface.

**Bounded question:** In interpreting our failed reversal result, what single control would best distinguish weak cue sampling from failure of a learned preference to update?

Dear Professor Ruta,

I’m an independent student researcher working on a sparse, from-scratch simulation constrained by the adult *Drosophila* connectome. I read your study with Annie Handler on temporal sensitivity in associative learning, especially the finding that changing cue–dopamine timing can reverse learned attraction on a trial-by-trial basis. That gives me a useful biological reference point for a problem in our current prototype.

In an engineered two-cue experiment, rewarded preference strengthened in all six seeds, while only three chose correctly after the reward contingency changed. The prespecified acceptance gate failed. Our rule does not model reward omission, and we have not identified a natural chess-reward pathway. If you had to recommend one control or measurement to distinguish inadequate new cue experience from deficient updating of the old association, what would be most informative?
