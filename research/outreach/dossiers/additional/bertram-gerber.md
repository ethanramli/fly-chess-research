## Research examined

### 1. Reinforcement signaling of punishment versus relief in fruit flies

2018, Learning & Memory. [Primary work / author institutional record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5959229/).

Defined dopamine-neuron stimulation supports different learned valences depending on cue timing. Punishment and relief effects are not equally dependent on dopamine biosynthesis, restricting a one-signal interpretation.

### 2. Functional architecture of reward learning in mushroom body extrinsic neurons of larval Drosophila

2018, Nature Communications. [Primary work / author institutional record](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-03130-1).

Single-cell anatomy, connectomics and targeted manipulations distinguish neuron contributions to odor preference, taste preference and associative learning in stage-3 larvae.

## Reasoned fit to FlyChess

His work helps prevent our existing temporal response reversal from being confused with successful contingency reversal. It also treats the behavioral meaning of experimentally stimulated neurons as a question to test.

**Bounded question:** What behavioral control would distinguish a timing-induced valence change from adaptation to a newly reversed contingency?

**Possible next diagnostic, proposed only:** Proposed design: orthogonally vary cue/stimulation order and which cue's action earns reinforcement, keeping informative-feedback and pulse-count controls separate. Freeze this new test before running it.

**Limits and reasons this may be the wrong contact:** The timing study's stimulation meaning is itself qualified; its identified cells and larval anatomy do not provide an adult gamma4 or chess mapping.

The current six-seed follow-up improved rewarded preference in 6/6 but reversed correctly in 3/6. Both completed acceptance protocols failed. The mechanism remains biologically unvalidated and the chess round records feedback without learning.
