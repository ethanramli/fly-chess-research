## Why relevant

The Branson Lab develops machine-vision and machine-learning methods for extracting quantitative behavioral structure from large neuroscience datasets. That expertise is relevant to operationalizing choice, feedback, and transfer as measurable behaviors rather than treating a model response change as sufficient evidence of learning. [4]

## Current/recent work

- Branson coauthored “Whole-body simulation of realistic fruit fly locomotion with deep reinforcement learning,” published in *Nature* in July 2025. The work combines an anatomical fly-body model and physics simulation with learned locomotion controllers; it is a body-and-control simulation, not a learned fly-brain connectome. [5]
- In May 2026, Branson and Ling-Qi Zhang posted “Neurodata without boredom: Benchmarking Agentic AI for data reuse,” a preprint on neuroscience-data reuse and documentation. [6]

## Specific overlap with this project

Researcher-provided project context: the project is an 8-GB-constrained, from-scratch simulation on fixed fly wiring. Its engineered gamma4 association prototype failed the frozen full acceptance gate because only 3/6 seeds selected the new option after reversal; neural input/action/reward mappings and chess transfer remain open. Branson's behavioral measurement expertise could help sharpen independent behavioral outcomes and distinguish choice behavior from an engineered neural-response proxy. [4]

## What to ask

What is one compact, preregisterable behavioral metric or control that would best distinguish genuine contingency updating from a fixed decoder's response drift in a small computational choice experiment?

## Reasons they may not be the best contact

Her lab is primarily a computational behavior-analysis and machine-vision group; it does not appear to specialize in biological reward pathways or synaptic learning rules. The 2025 fly locomotion paper uses deep reinforcement learning for body control, which is distinct from the project's fixed-connectome biological-learning objective. [4,5]
