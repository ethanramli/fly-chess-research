## Why relevant

The Jayaraman Lab studies how fly central-complex circuits combine sensory, self-motion, and past-experience signals to support adaptive action and navigation. This is especially relevant to the project's unresolved sensory/action mappings and to separating a fixed choice readout from a biologically supported action-selection pathway. [3]

## Current/recent work

- In 2023, Jayaraman coauthored “Converting an allocentric goal into an egocentric steering signal,” which analyzed how fly central-complex circuits transform heading and goal representations into steering-related output. [4]
- The lab's 2025 Cold Spring Harbor Drosophila meeting abstract book includes a Jayaraman coauthored project with Pavithraa Seenivasan, Judith Hoeller, and Edward Rogers on neural representations used during navigation. The abstract is early-stage conference material, not a peer-reviewed article. [5]

## Specific overlap with this project

Researcher-provided context: the project aims to test learning from puzzle choices and feedback while preserving connectome wiring. The feedback receiver currently records events without applying neural learning, and its one-puzzle fixture is only task-plumbing evidence. The gamma4 choice experiments do not resolve natural action/reward interfaces, and reversal passed in only 3/6 seeds. Jayaraman's circuit-level work is useful for identifying the gap between a simulated choice variable and a real sensorimotor pathway. [3,4]

## Reasons they may not be the best contact

The lab's work centers on navigation and central-complex sensorimotor computations, not chess or mushroom-body reward learning. The project's current mechanism is in gamma4 mushroom-body circuitry, so a specialist in associative learning may better address the plasticity question. [3,4]
