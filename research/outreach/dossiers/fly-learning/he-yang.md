## Why relevant

Yang's 2025 paper studies how prior aversive or appetitive experience changes responses and preference for a later novel odor through mushroom-body circuitry. It is pertinent to context, stimulus specificity, and persistent preference, all important cautions for the project's simplified cue and reversal experiment. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.107736)

## Specific overlap with this project

The project uses a simplified two-cue task; Yang's work highlights that prior experience may alter responses to novel stimuli through interacting memory circuits. That makes transfer and cue generalization important tests, not assumptions. The project's full reversal gate failed at 3/6, and chess transfer remains untested. [Project validation](../../../../documentation/design/neural-learning/gamma4-association-tests.md) · [feedback design](../../../../documentation/design/research-goals/feedback-learning.md)
