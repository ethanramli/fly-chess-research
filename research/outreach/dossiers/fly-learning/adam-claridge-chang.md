## Why relevant

His work connects Drosophila associative learning, neuromodulators, mushroom-body circuits, and methods for optogenetically controlling neural activity. Those are directly relevant to the project's still-unresolved biological reward interface and to the distinction between a teacher's feedback and a neural mechanism. This is advice on biological plausibility, not a claim that his lab studies chess or connectome-constrained simulation. [Lab research](https://claridgechang.com/index.html)

## Specific overlap with this project

The lab's work on dopamine-neuron function, synthetic memory induction, and behavioral assays could inform what counts as a defensible reward signal and how to separate memory change from immediate behavioral effects. The project's gamma4 simulation remains an engineered association with failed full reversal acceptance; natural reward/sensory/action mappings and chess transfer remain unresolved. [2025–26 project/papers](https://claridgechang.com/papers/) · [project feedback design](../../../../documentation/design/research-goals/feedback-learning.md) · [association validation](../../../../documentation/design/neural-learning/gamma4-association-tests.md)

## What to ask

For a small connectome-constrained model with a mapped γ4 compartment but no validated natural chess-reward pathway, what single experiment would best distinguish genuine reward-contingent memory updating from an imposed stimulation association?

## Reasons they may not be the best contact

The lab spans several neuroscience and tool-development topics, and a high-school computational project is distant from its experimental systems. If the immediate need is a detailed account of synaptic learning rules or a quick model-level critique, a postdoc or researcher working directly on the relevant circuit may be a more practical first contact. ## Personalized email draft

**Subject:** A bounded question about reward-contingent learning in a fly connectome model

Dear Professor Claridge-Chang,

I’m a [grade/year] high-school student working independently on a sparse, connectome-constrained Drosophila simulation that I hope to use for puzzle-based chess learning. I want to be precise about what the current model shows: our engineered γ4 association prototype changed cue preference, but full acceptance failed because only three of six seeds selected the reversed contingency. Natural sensory, reward, and action mappings, as well as chess transfer, are still unresolved.
