## Why relevant

Aso's lab studies how mushroom-body memory units store information using different learning rules and how distributed memories combine to guide action selection. Its stated work directly concerns the plasticity, memory persistence, and choice questions that remain unresolved in this project. [Aso Lab research](https://www.janelia.org/lab/aso-lab)

## Specific overlap with this project

The Aso Lab's γ-compartment work is relevant to the existing engineered gamma4 prototype, particularly how compartment-specific dopamine signals, memory updating, decay, and action selection should be represented. The project must not transfer those findings to chess as if a natural chess-reward mapping had been established; its reversal validation remains failed at 3/6 and chess transfer is untested. [Aso Lab overview](https://www.janelia.org/lab/aso-lab) · [project design](../../../../documentation/design/research-goals/feedback-learning.md) · [validation record](../../../../documentation/design/neural-learning/gamma4-association-tests.md)

## What to ask

Given a mapped γ4 compartment and a simulation that strengthens rewarded cue preference but reverses correctly in only 3/6 seeds, what one biological constraint or control should guide the next reversal diagnosis?

## Reasons they may not be the best contact

The lab focuses on experimental molecular and circuit mechanisms in the mushroom body; this project is a small independent computational effort and has not established a biological reward or sensory pathway. A postdoctoral scientist or research specialist may be more accessible for a tightly scoped question about particular circuits, tools, or papers.
