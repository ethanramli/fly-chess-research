# Potential mentors and research hubs

**Prepared:** 2026-09-28  
**Status:** Initial outreach list; verify every affiliation, role, email address, and current availability before contacting anyone.

## Purpose

This guide identifies researchers whose work overlaps with parts of the project:

- Drosophila connectomics;
- neural circuits for learning, memory, value, and behavior;
- computational neuroscience and machine learning;
- cognitive modeling and strategic decision-making.

No researcher listed here has agreed to supervise this project. These are potential contacts for methodological feedback, formal supervision, student-program advice, or referrals. A faculty member may be unable to mentor an independent researcher, may require enrollment in a university program, or may redirect inquiries to a postdoctoral researcher or administrator.

## Important outreach principles

1. Use the person's current official institutional profile as the source of truth.
2. Verify the email address immediately before sending; do not rely on copied lists.
3. Contact a small number of highly relevant researchers with individualized messages rather than sending a mass email.
4. Refer to one specific paper, dataset, or research program from the person's current work.
5. Ask for a bounded request: for example, whether they could suggest a suitable mentor, identify a major methodological flaw, or recommend a relevant lab.
6. Do not assume that a prominent professor can provide one-to-one supervision.
7. Do not claim that a researcher endorses the project or shares its conclusions.
8. Explain that the project is a computational model inspired by a fly connectome, not a claim that biological flies understand chess.
9. Keep a record of contact date, response, referral, and any agreed expectations.
10. Discuss authorship, access, intellectual property, and the scope of any collaboration before representing someone as a mentor or collaborator.

## What the researchers have in common

The overlap is a research orientation, not a guarantee of shared conclusions:

- They study neural computation through identifiable circuits, behavior, or representations.
- Several work with Drosophila connectomes or neural circuits for learning and decision-making.
- Their work links biological data to computational or mechanistic explanations rather than treating performance as a black-box benchmark alone.
- They generally work in collaborative, interdisciplinary environments involving neuroscience, computation, imaging, genetics, or machine learning.
- Their research programs emphasize measurement, mechanism, and reproducibility.

This commonality explains why they may be useful contacts. It does **not** establish their individual mentoring style, willingness to supervise an unaffiliated student, or agreement with the cognitive-science framing. Assess mentoring fit from current lab pages, trainee experiences, publications, and an actual conversation.

## Recommended contact order

1. Start with one or two researchers whose work most directly matches the immediate question, rather than emailing everyone simultaneously.
2. Consider **Mala Murthy** or **Glenn Turner** for fly learning, circuits, and behavior.
3. Consider **Sebastian Seung** for computational connectomics and methodological feedback.
4. Consider **Marta Zlatic**, **Albert Cardona**, or **Scott Waddell** for circuit-level learning, value, memory, and connectomics.
5. Consider **Gregory Jefferis** for connectome interpretation and the FlyWire/connectomics context.
6. Ask a school counselor, science teacher, or local university outreach office to help identify a nearby faculty member or formal research-mentorship program. A local mentor may be more realistically available than a globally prominent PI.

This ordering is about topical fit, not prestige or likelihood of acceptance.

## Outreach email template

**Subject:** Request for brief guidance on connectome-constrained cognitive modeling

> Dear Professor [Surname],
>
> I am an independent researcher developing a computational cognitive-science project on whether a model constrained by a Drosophila connectome can learn structured chess-position evaluation and generalize to unfamiliar positions. Chess is being used as a controlled testbed; I am not claiming that biological flies understand chess.
>
> I read your work on [specific paper, dataset, or research program], particularly [one concrete detail]. It is relevant to my question about [learning/value/circuit computation/connectome modeling].
>
> I have drafted a protocol with held-out tests and ablations intended to separate the constrained model, training signal, external search, and interface. I am not asking you to take responsibility for the project. If you have time, would you be willing to suggest whether the question is scientifically well-posed, recommend a more suitable mentor or lab, or point me toward a key paper I should not miss?
>
> I can send a one-page summary and protocol if useful. I understand completely if your schedule or institutional policy prevents you from responding.
>
> Best regards,  
> [Full name]  
> [School or independent-research status]  
> [Location/time zone]  
> [GitHub or project link]

Do not attach a long document in the first message unless requested. A concise one-page summary and a stable project link are usually more effective.
