# Project overview

Lead researcher: Ethan Ramli, grade 11 student in Jakarta (self-reported). Primary framing: cognitive science with computational neuroscience and machine-learning methods. Target output: transparent empirical research report; publication venue remains to be selected.

The user-confirmed overarching question is whether a simple organism can achieve reasoning. Operationally, this project tests whether a Drosophila-connectome-constrained computational model, initialized from scratch, can develop consequence-sensitive chess evaluation through experience-dependent plasticity and transfer it to unfamiliar/counterfactual chess positions. Chess is the testbed for transferable reasoning, not a claim about living-fly chess cognition or cross-domain general intelligence.

The researcher ultimately wants the model to develop its own evaluation/style and reach 2000 Elo. That is a long-term playing-strength aspiration requiring its own declared scale and calibrated test. The first learning work uses bounded puzzle/consequence tasks as a staged prerequisite, not as the final research scope. A human comparison can support a later comparative-performance claim.

The teacher supplies experiences and independently verified feedback rather than neural parameter updates. Symbolic board encoders and action readouts are engineered; natural visual perception and biological validity remain unresolved. Some controlled computational association prerequisites pass, but useful puzzle learning and reasoning have not been established.
