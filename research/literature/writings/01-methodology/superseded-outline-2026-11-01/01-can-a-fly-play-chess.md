# Chapter 1: Can a Fly Play Chess?

**Volume:** [Volume 1: Methodology](../README.md)  
**Status:** Planning document; chapter draft not yet written.

## Purpose

Open with the moment the project began and explain how the original curiosity became a research programme.

## Planned content

- While preparing for a chess tournament, the researcher found an openly available fly connectome and wondered whether a fly could play chess.
- The initial idea was to train a simulated fly brain with reinforcement learning: reward good moves and punish bad ones, initially imagined as dopamine.
- The deeper motivation was whether a small organism could think for itself after enough training.
- Finding Cesp's fly-chess project sharpened the question: chess-playing alone did not answer whether the system could produce novel solutions from experience.
- The researcher's intended reasoning test became applying multiple unrelated concepts, such as tactics, king safety, opening principles and endgame patterns, to unfamiliar positions.

## Evidence boundary

Present the backpropagation concern as the researcher's motivation, not as proof that backpropagation necessarily produces memorization. Distinguish a computational model from a living fly.
